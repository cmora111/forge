# CANDIDATE-LAW-0002

## Constitutional Law II — Procedural Authority

**Status:** Candidate

---

## Purpose

This candidate law defines the constitutional constraints governing the
origin, scope, exercise, transition, and representation of Procedural
Authority within the Forge–Stage Method.

It does not identify or appoint a holder of Originating Procedural Authority.

It does not, merely by existing, establish the authority required for its own
constitutional adoption.

Normative language within this candidate expresses the proposed
constitutional effect of legitimate adoption.

While this artifact remains a Candidate, that normative language does not
itself exercise constitutional authority.

---

## Origin

This candidate law arose from investigation of the procedural authority
required to perform normative lifecycle transitions within the Forge–Stage
Method.

That investigation exposed an unresolved dependency concerning the origin and
legitimacy of the Forge's first Originating Procedural Authority.

Forge investigation produced candidate requirements for a constituting
authority provenance model.

Those requirements were subjected to Anvil inspection.

The Anvil hardened the model against cases involving:

- multiple originating authority holders;
- death, incapacity, resignation, and authority continuity;
- delegation and revocation;
- succession and competing succession claims;
- canonical Method identity and forks;
- institutional authority;
- constitutional amendment;
- self-expansion of delegated authority;
- historical uncertainty;
- constitutive versus provenance evidence; and
- retroactive application of later governance.

The hardened requirements were returned to the Forge and consolidated into
the five candidate constitutional principles below.

---

# Principle I — Authority Requires Provenance

Every exercise of Procedural Authority SHALL be traceable to a legitimate
authority source.

For Originating Procedural Authority, the legitimate authority basis may be
logically prior to the procedural system constituted under that authority and
need not derive from a pre-existing Forge procedural authority.

Authority provenance SHALL establish, as applicable:

- the authority basis;
- the authority holder or holders;
- the manner in which authority may be exercised;
- the constituting, delegating, transferring, or succeeding act; and
- the evidence supporting the claimed authority lineage.

Confidence in historical authority provenance SHALL NOT exceed the evidence
that survives legitimate inspection.

An unresolved provenance gap SHALL remain explicitly unresolved and SHALL NOT
be represented as established authority.

---

# Principle II — Authority Is Bounded

Procedural Authority SHALL possess only the scope and jurisdiction
legitimately established by its authority source.

No authority holder SHALL enlarge its own authority merely by assertion,
exercise, delegation, status, or representation.

No delegation SHALL convey authority exceeding that legitimately possessed by
its source.

Possession of Procedural Authority SHALL NOT by itself confer Engineering
Authority.

Authority over the Forge–Stage Method SHALL NOT by itself establish
jurisdiction over independent engineering work or other domains not
legitimately subjected to that authority.

---

# Principle III — Authority Transitions Require Derivability

Every change in Procedural Authority SHALL be derivable from the authoritative
state immediately preceding it through a transition authorized to produce the
resulting authority state.

This requirement applies, as appropriate, to:

- delegation;
- revocation;
- succession;
- transfer;
- replacement;
- continuity; and
- other changes in authority state.

Delegation and succession SHALL remain distinguishable.

A delegation permits another authorized holder or actor to exercise authority
within the delegated scope.

A succession or transfer changes the holder of the authority itself according
to governing authority.

A claimed authority transition SHALL NOT acquire legitimacy merely because
the transition is asserted or recorded.

Competing claims to authority SHALL NOT silently create multiple legitimate
authority holders unless the governing authority explicitly permits such a
result.

Canonical Forge–Stage Method authority SHALL remain traceable through
legitimate constituting and subsequent authority transitions.

A derivative or fork SHALL NOT acquire canonical authority merely by copying,
modifying, or claiming the identity of the canonical Method.

---

# Principle IV — Constituted Governance Binds Its Exercise

Procedural Authority placed under legitimately constituted governance SHALL
be exercised according to that governance until the governing state is itself
legitimately amended, superseded, or terminated.

Originating status SHALL NOT provide a bypass around governance validly
constituted under that authority.

An authority holder wishing to alter a governing constraint SHALL use the
authority and procedure legitimately established for amendment, supersession,
or termination of that constraint.

Future constitutional change SHALL derive from the authoritative
constitutional state immediately preceding the change.

Later governance SHALL NOT be retroactively imposed as though it governed an
originating act that preceded that governance.

Historical authority SHALL instead be evaluated according to the legitimate
authority conditions applicable when the relevant act occurred.

---

# Principle V — Representation Does Not Create Authority

No artifact, status, record, declaration, label, or other representation
SHALL acquire or confer Procedural Authority merely by asserting the
existence, possession, exercise, transfer, or result of that authority.

The representation of an authoritative act SHALL remain distinguishable from
the authoritative act itself.

Where an artifact participates in constitution, delegation, succession,
approval, or another authoritative act, its authority derives from the
legitimate act under which the artifact becomes authoritative and not merely
from the words contained within the artifact.

An artifact that records historical authority provenance SHALL NOT represent
later memorialization as though it were contemporaneous evidence of an
earlier constituting act.

Memorialization SHALL NOT manufacture a historical authority event that
cannot legitimately be established.

---

# Derived Requirements

The five principles above govern the more detailed requirements discovered
during Forge investigation and Anvil inspection.

These include:

1. authority basis;
2. originating authority holder or holders;
3. authority exercise model;
4. constituting act;
5. historical provenance;
6. historical confidence and uncertainty;
7. scope and jurisdiction;
8. explicit limits;
9. canonical Method identity;
10. delegation;
11. delegation persistence and revocation;
12. prohibition on self-expansion;
13. succession and transfer;
14. authority continuity;
15. competing-succession resolution;
16. governance supremacy;
17. constitutional evolution;
18. distinction between constitutive and provenance evidence;
19. non-retroactivity; and
20. non-expansion of jurisdiction.

These derived requirements SHALL NOT be interpreted in a manner inconsistent
with the five constitutional principles from which they derive.

# Historical Boundary

This candidate law does not determine as historical fact:

- who first held Originating Procedural Authority for the Forge;
- the precise act by which that authority was first exercised;
- whether that authority was individual, joint, institutional, or otherwise
  constituted;
- whether existing historical approval or adoption acts possess complete
  authority provenance; or
- whether a presently valid delegation or succession of that authority
  already exists.

Those questions require evidence.

Absence of presently established provenance SHALL NOT be represented as proof
that legitimate historical authority did not exist.

Likewise, historical practice SHALL NOT by itself be represented as proof
that legitimate authority did exist.

The confidence warranted by the surviving evidence SHALL govern any such
historical determination.

---

# Constitutional Boundary

This document is a candidate constitutional artifact.

Its creation records the result of Forge investigation and Anvil-hardened
constitutional reasoning.

Its existence SHALL NOT be interpreted as:

- constitutional adoption;
- creation of Originating Procedural Authority;
- delegation of Procedural Authority;
- approval of this candidate law;
- amendment of the Forge Constitution; or
- authority to perform any other normative lifecycle transition.

The legitimate procedure and authority required to adopt this candidate as
constitutional law remain subject to the very provenance requirements stated
here.
