# FD-0002 — Investigation Pattern Recognition

**Status:** Accepted  
**Version:** 0.1.0

---

# Summary

Following the discovery of Engineering Tension (FD-0001), the next
engineering question became how the Forge determines which question
should be asked next.

This investigation discovered that recurring forms of engineering
tension produce recurring patterns of investigation.

Recognition of these investigation patterns enables the engineer to
select the class of question most capable of reducing the recognized
engineering tension.

Question selection therefore becomes a disciplined engineering process
rather than an intuitive activity.

---

# Context

Following the discovery of Engineering Tension (FD-0001), a new
engineering question emerged.

If engineering tension explains why the Forge begins, what determines
which question should be asked next?

Repeated Forge investigations showed that similar engineering tensions
consistently produced similar classes of engineering questions.

This observation suggested that Forge investigations follow recognizable
patterns rather than arbitrary sequences of questions.

---

# Engineering Tension

Engineers frequently recognize that further investigation is required,
yet many possible questions appear available.

Without a disciplined method of selecting the next question,
investigation becomes inconsistent, inefficient, and dependent upon
individual intuition.

The engineering tension therefore became:

> How does the Forge determine which question should be asked next?

---

# Forge Investigation

The Forge examined numerous engineering investigations conducted during
development of the Forge–Stage Method.

Each investigation was inspected to determine why a particular question
had been selected.

The investigation repeatedly observed that the question was determined
by the recognized engineering tension rather than by personal preference.

Recurring investigation patterns began to emerge.

---

# Discovery

> **The Forge does not choose its next question arbitrarily.**

The Forge recognizes the investigation pattern represented by the 
engineering tension and selects the class of question most capable
of reducing that tension.

Recurring forms of engineering tension therefore produce recurring
patterns of investigation.

Recognition of these recurring investigation patterns provides a
disciplined method for selecting the next Forge question.

---

# Recurring Investigation Patterns

The following recurring investigation patterns have 
been observed during development of the Forge–Stage Method.

| Engineering Tension | Typical Forge Question |
|----------------------|------------------------|
| Responsibility | Who owns this responsibility? |
| Authority | What gives this authority? |
| Dependency | What must exist before this? |
| Necessity | Why must this exist? |
| Universality | Is this universally true? |
| Ambiguity | Can this be interpreted differently? |
| Duplication | Why do two things appear to own this? |
| Exception | Why is special treatment required? |

These patterns represent observations rather than a complete taxonomy.

Additional recurring investigation patterns may emerge through future engineering
experience.

---

# Bedrock

Bedrock was reached when the investigation found that question selection
could consistently be explained by the type of engineering uncertainty
being investigated.

No more fundamental mechanism was discovered governing question
selection.

Engineering tension determines that investigation is required.

Recognition of the recurring investigation pattern determines how the investigation proceeds.

---

# Rationale

Without recognizable recurring investigation patterns, effective questioning
remains dependent upon experience and intuition.

By recognizing recurring investigation patterns, the Forge transforms
question selection into a repeatable engineering discipline.

This discovery explains why experienced investigations often appear
systematic rather than exploratory.

The apparent intuition is actually recognition of recurring engineering
patterns.

---

# Implications

This discovery establishes that:

- recognition of investigation patterns can be taught;
- investigation quality can be inspected;
- recurring engineering uncertainty produces recurring investigative behavior;
- Forge investigations become reproducible;
- additional recurring investigation patterns may be discovered through continued
  engineering practice.

This discovery provides the foundation for:

- Dependency Tracing;
- Bedrock Recognition;
- the Forge Decision Process;
- future Forge training.
