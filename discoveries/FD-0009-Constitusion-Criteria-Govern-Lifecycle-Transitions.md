# FD-0009

Constitutional Completion Criteria Govern Lifecycle Transitions

Status

Draft

---

# Context

Investigation of the Knowledge Lifecycle sought to determine what constitutionally permits progression from one lifecycle stage to the next.

The investigation began while examining lifecycle governance within the Stage Method and determining the legitimacy of lifecycle transitions.

---

# Engineering Tension

Lifecycle stages clearly transition.

The constitutional mechanism permitting those transitions was not explicitly understood.

Several possible mechanisms were considered, including:

- elapsed time,
- engineering effort,
- accumulated evidence,
- changing authority.

The Knowledge Lifecycle specification was inspected to determine which mechanism was constitutionally supported.

---

# Forge Investigation

The investigation examined the constitutional responsibilities assigned to:

- Observation
- Question
- Investigation
- Discovery
- Repeated Validation
- Candidate Principle

Inspection focused upon:

- responsibility,
- evidentiary conditions,
- authority,
- lifecycle progression.

Candidate explanations were repeatedly refined through dependency inspection until unsupported explanations were eliminated.

---

# Discovery

Lifecycle transitions are permitted only when the constitutional completion criteria of the current lifecycle stage have been satisfied.

Lifecycle progression is therefore governed by constitutional completion rather than by elapsed time, engineering effort, accumulated evidence, or preference.

---

# Rationale

The Knowledge Lifecycle consistently associates each stage with:

- a distinct responsibility,
- a distinct evidentiary condition,
- a distinct form of authority.

Inspection demonstrated that advancement depends upon satisfaction of the governing constitutional requirements of the current stage.

No evidence was found supporting lifecycle advancement based solely upon time, work performed, or subjective completion.

---

# Implications

This discovery explains why:

- investigations may suspend without conclusion,
- questions possess no authority,
- repeated validation precedes candidate principles,
- procedural adoption remains distinct from engineering justification.

The discovery also supports the observed behavior of the Stage Method, where engineering stages legitimately advance only after their constitutional responsibilities have been discharged.
