# FD-0009 — Lifecycle Transition Governance

**Status:** Accepted
**Version:** 0.1.0

---

# Context

Investigation of the Knowledge Lifecycle sought to determine what constitutionally permits progression from one lifecycle stage to the next.

The investigation originated while examining lifecycle governance within the Stage Method and determining the legitimacy of stage transitions.

---

# Engineering Tension

Lifecycle stages clearly progress through the Knowledge Lifecycle.

The constitutional mechanism governing those transitions was not explicitly understood.

Several possible mechanisms were considered, including:

- elapsed time,
- engineering effort,
- accumulated evidence,
- changing authority.

Inspection of the Knowledge Lifecycle specification was performed to determine which mechanism was constitutionally supported.

---

# Forge Investigation

The investigation examined the constitutional definitions of:

- Observation
- Question
- Investigation
- Discovery
- Repeated Validation
- Candidate Principle

The dependency chain focused on the relationship between:

- stage responsibility,
- evidentiary conditions,
- engineering justification,
- authority,
- lifecycle progression.

Successive candidate explanations were refined until unsupported dependencies were eliminated.

---

# Discovery

Lifecycle transitions are permitted only after the constitutional responsibility of the current lifecycle stage has been legitimately discharged.

## Scope

This discovery describes the constitutional behavior of the Knowledge Lifecycle.

It does not prescribe engineering procedure.

Any procedural requirements derived from this discovery require subsequent lifecycle progression.

---

# Bedrock

Lifecycle transitions are governed by the legitimate discharge of the constitutional responsibility assigned to the current lifecycle stage.

The transition itself is not the constitutional event.

The constitutional event is the legitimate discharge of the responsibility.

The transition is its consequence.

---

# Rationale

## Observed

The Knowledge Lifecycle explicitly assigns each stage:

- a distinct responsibility,
- a distinct evidentiary condition,
- a distinct form of authority (or explicit absence of authority).

The specification explicitly requires fulfillment of stage responsibilities before advancement.

---

## Inferred

Inspection suggests that lifecycle transitions are therefore consequences of legitimate discharge of stage responsibilities rather than independent constitutional events.

---

# Implications

This discovery explains why:

- Questions possess no engineering or procedural authority.
- Investigations may suspend without producing Discovery.
- Discovery requires justified engineering conclusions.
- Repeated Validation precedes Candidate Principle.
- Procedural Adoption remains distinct from Engineering Authority.

This discovery is consistent with the suspension of Stage 3 pending completion of Stage 2.
