# FD-0015 — Originating Procedural Authority

**Status:** Hold
**Version:** 0.1.0

---

# Context

The Forge–Stage Method did not begin as a pre-existing institutional
governance system.

It emerged during engineering work in which a practitioner responsible for
that work began imposing methodological constraints upon how investigation
would proceed.

The Stage Method developed from those constraints, and the Forge later
emerged as questions concerning the Method and its foundations were exposed.

As the Method became increasingly formal, a deeper authority question arose:

> By what authority could methodological constraints have been established
> before a formal constitutional governance system existed?

Requiring prior institutional governance for the first legitimate
methodological constraint would create a circular dependency:

```text
procedural authority
        ↓
requires governance
        ↓
governance
        ↓
requires prior procedural authority
```

---

# Discovery

Originating Procedural Authority need not originate from a prior formal
constitutional system.

Where a practitioner possesses legitimate responsibility for engineering work,
that responsibility may provide the basis for bounded authority to determine
the methodological conditions under which that work will be performed.

Therefore:

> Legitimate responsibility for engineering work may provide the basis for
> originating procedural authority over the methodology governing that work.

This authority is local and bounded.

It does not arise merely because a practitioner declares authority, nor does
responsibility for one engineering domain create universal methodological
jurisdiction.

The authority derives from the practitioner's legitimate responsibility for
the work within which the methodological discipline originates.

---

# Authority Derivation

The discovered relationship is:

    Legitimate Engineering Responsibility
                    │
                    ▼
    Authority to Govern the Conduct
    of That Engineering Work
                    │
                    ▼
    Methodological Constraints
                    │
                    ▼
    Originating Procedural Authority
    within the Responsible Domain

The originating authority is therefore not self-created.

Its basis precedes the methodological rules that are established through it.

---

# Scope

Originating Procedural Authority is bounded by the responsibility from which
it derives.

Therefore:

    responsibility for domain X
            ↓
    may support procedural authority
    for engineering activity within X

but does not establish:

    responsibility for domain X
            ↓
    universal procedural authority
    over unrelated domains

Canonical authority over a Method that emerges from such work likewise does
not, by itself, create jurisdiction over independent engineering activity.

Canonical identity and jurisdiction are distinct.

---

# Incremental Emergence

The existence of originating procedural authority does not require a single
ceremonial declaration establishing the Method.

A methodological discipline may emerge incrementally as legitimate
constraints are established, exercised, tested, preserved, and formalized
within the responsible engineering activity.

Therefore the absence of a historical declaration such as:

> "The Method is hereby constituted."

does not by itself establish that the Method lacked legitimate originating
procedural authority.

However, repeated behavior or voluntary compliance alone does not prove
authority.

The legitimacy of the originating procedural authority depends upon the
underlying responsibility from which the methodological governance arose.

---

# Canonical Identity Boundary

A Method that emerges within legitimately governed engineering work may later
develop an identifiable canonical form and continuity.

The existence of bounded Originating Procedural Authority does not by itself
establish the full authority basis for governing that later canonical identity.

Nor does canonical identity, once established, create jurisdiction over
independent engineering domains.

Therefore:

    Originating Procedural Authority
                ≠
    Universal Methodological Jurisdiction

and:

    Canonical Identity
                ≠
    External Jurisdiction

The authority governing the continuing canonical identity of an established
Method requires its own derivation where that authority extends beyond the
original engineering responsibility from which the Method emerged.

---

# Institutionalization Boundary

Originating Procedural Authority and institutionalized procedural governance
are not identical.

Originating authority may exist before institutional governance is
constituted.

A later transition may place future exercises of canonical procedural
authority under institutional governance.

That transition is a separate authority problem and is not established merely
by the existence of originating procedural authority.

Therefore this discovery does not itself establish:

- a Constituting Artifact,
- institutionalized constitutional governance,
- a constitutional authority transition,
- or authority for any proposed Constitutional Law.

Those require separate derivation.

---

# Consequence

The absence of prior formal constitutional governance does not necessarily
create an authority void at the origin of a Method.

Where legitimate engineering responsibility supports bounded authority over
the conduct of that work, methodological governance may legitimately originate
within that responsibility.

This provides a non-circular authority basis from which a Method may begin
before later institutionalization becomes possible.

---

# Does Not Establish

This discovery does not establish:

- universal authority of the originating practitioner,
- authority outside the originating responsibility domain,
- that every methodological decision made by an originator is legitimate,
- that repeated practice alone creates authority,
- that canonical identity creates external jurisdiction,
- that institutionalization has already occurred,
- the contents or authority of a Constituting Artifact,
- or the legitimacy of any later constitutional transition.
