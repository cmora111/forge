# FD-0016 — Canonical Method Stewardship Authority

**Status:** Hold
**Version:** 0.1.0

---

# Context

FD-0015 establishes that legitimate responsibility for engineering work may
provide the basis for bounded Originating Procedural Authority over the
methodology governing that work.

It also establishes an important boundary.

Originating Procedural Authority does not by itself establish universal
methodological jurisdiction, nor does the emergence of canonical identity
automatically establish authority over that continuing identity.

As methodological discipline becomes preserved, reused, and increasingly
formalized, however, the governed methodology may become identifiable as a
Method in its own right.

This exposes a further authority question:

> Under what conditions can authority that legitimately governed the
> originating methodological activity continue as authority to preserve and
> govern the canonical identity and knowledge corpus of the Method that
> emerged from that activity?

The answer cannot be that authority simply expands because the Method grows.

Nor can authorship, repository ownership, popularity, repeated practice, or
voluntary adoption independently create such authority.

The relationship between Originating Procedural Authority and continuing
canonical Method governance therefore requires its own derivation.

---

# Discovery

Canonical Method Stewardship Authority may exist as a continuing expression
of an existing bounded Originating Procedural Authority when the methodology
governed under that authority becomes an identifiable Method.

This continuity is legitimate only where the stewardship remains within the
continuing scope of preserving and governing the Method that emerged from the
originally governed methodological activity.

Therefore:

> The emergence of an identifiable Method need not require an expansion of
> Procedural Authority where governance of that Method remains traceably
> continuous in origin, identity, scope, and provenance with the
> methodological activity already governed by legitimate Originating
> Procedural Authority.

The Method becoming identifiable changes the object being preserved.

It does not, by itself, enlarge the jurisdiction of the authority governing
that object.

---

# Authority Continuity

The discovered relationship is:

    Legitimate Engineering Responsibility
                    │
                    ▼
    Originating Procedural Authority
                    │
                    ▼
    Governed Methodological Activity
                    │
                    ▼
    Preserved Methodological Discipline
                    │
                    ▼
    Identifiable Method
                    │
                    ▼
    Continuity of:
      - Origin
      - Identity
      - Scope
      - Provenance
                    │
                    ▼
    Canonical Method Stewardship Authority

Canonical Method Stewardship Authority therefore does not arise merely from
creation of a Method.

Its legitimacy depends upon continuity with the authority relationship under
which the Method emerged.

---

# Continuity Conditions

Canonical Method Stewardship Authority requires the following conditions.

## Legitimate Origin

The methodological activity from which the Method emerged must have been
governed by legitimate bounded Procedural Authority.

An illegitimate authority claim cannot become legitimate merely because the
resulting methodology later becomes identifiable.

## Identity Continuity

The Method being stewarded must be traceably continuous with the methodology
that developed under the originating authority.

Authority over one methodology does not establish authority over an unrelated
Method merely because the same practitioner created both.

## Scope Continuity

Stewardship must remain within the continuing governance of the Method that
emerged from the originally governed methodological activity.

The Method becoming larger, more formal, more widely used, or more important
does not by itself expand the scope of the underlying authority.

## Provenance Continuity

The relationship between the originating methodological activity, the
emerging Method, and the continuing canonical lineage must remain inspectably
traceable.

Canonical identity cannot legitimately depend upon an untraceable assertion
of continuity.

---

# Canonical Stewardship Scope

Within those boundaries, Canonical Method Stewardship Authority may govern
the preservation and continuing identity of the canonical Method.

Its possible scope includes matters concerning:

- canonical Method identity,
- canonical lineage,
- preservation of Method artifacts,
- organization of the Method's knowledge corpus,
- identification of artifacts belonging to the canonical lineage,
- and controlled evolution of that canonical corpus within existing
  authority.

These are acts concerning the Method itself.

They do not establish authority over independent engineering domains or over
persons merely because those persons use, study, implement, reject, or modify
the Method.

---

# Canonical Identity Is Not External Jurisdiction

Canonical Method Stewardship Authority distinguishes authority over a Method
from authority over users of that Method.

Therefore:

    Authority over Canonical Method Identity
                    ≠
    Authority over Method Users

and:

    Canonical Method Stewardship
                    ≠
    Universal Methodological Jurisdiction

A person or organization outside the canonical authority relationship may:

- use the Method,
- reject the Method,
- modify the Method,
- fork the Method,
- or develop another Method.

Canonical stewardship does not prohibit those acts.

It determines only what remains within the canonical lineage governed by that
stewardship authority.

---

# Derivative Methods

Independent modification does not automatically alter the canonical Method.

A derivative Method may legitimately exist outside the canonical lineage
without being governed by the canonical steward.

Therefore:

    Freedom to Derive or Fork
                ≠
    Authority to Redefine
    the Canonical Lineage

Likewise, popularity or widespread adoption of a derivative does not by itself
transfer canonical authority.

Canonical lineage and external adoption are distinct relationships.

---

# Knowledge Corpus

An identifiable Method may preserve a knowledge corpus concerning discoveries
made during its development and operation.

Where preservation of that corpus forms part of the continuing canonical
identity of the Method, governance of the corpus may fall within the scope of
Canonical Method Stewardship Authority.

However, Procedural Authority over the corpus does not create engineering
truth.

Therefore:

    Engineering Discovery
            ≠
    Procedural Acceptance

and:

    Procedural Acceptance
            ≠
    Creation of Truth

Engineering determination and Procedural Authority remain distinct.

The procedures and predicates by which a finding may legitimately enter the
canonical knowledge corpus require separate governance.

---

# Stewardship Is Not Sovereignty

Canonical Method Stewardship Authority is bounded authority.

It does not establish permanent personal sovereignty over the Method.

Stewardship may later be legitimately:

- constrained,
- delegated,
- transferred,
- institutionalized,
- superseded,
- or terminated

through an authorized governance transition.

The existence of pre-institutional stewardship therefore does not prevent the
Method from later placing canonical authority under institutional governance.

---

# Consequence

The emergence of an identifiable Method does not necessarily create an
authority gap between originating methodological governance and continuing
canonical stewardship.

Where legitimate Originating Procedural Authority already governs the
methodological activity from which the Method emerges, continuing authority
over the Method may remain legitimate without an expansion of jurisdiction
when origin, identity, scope, and provenance remain continuous.

This provides a possible authority bridge between:

    Originating Procedural Authority
                ↓
    Identifiable Method
                ↓
    Canonical Method Stewardship Authority

without converting canonical stewardship into authority over independent
users or unrelated engineering activity.

---

# Does Not Establish

This discovery does not establish:

- that authorship alone creates canonical authority,
- that repository ownership creates canonical authority,
- that popularity creates canonical authority,
- that repeated practice creates canonical authority,
- that voluntary adoption creates canonical authority,
- authority over independent Method users,
- authority over unrelated engineering domains,
- authority over derivative Methods outside the canonical lineage,
- that Procedural Authority can create engineering truth,
- an unrestricted right to alter the canonical Method,
- a specific Forge Discovery acceptance procedure,
- that Anvil verification itself constitutes Discovery acceptance,
- that any particular Discovery is Accepted,
- institutionalized constitutional governance,
- or permanent personal sovereignty over the Method.
