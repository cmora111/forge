# FD-0017 — Canonical Discovery Acceptance

**Status:** Hold
**Version:** 0.1.0

---

# Context

The Forge produces candidate discoveries concerning the engineering and
methodological relationships it investigates.

The Anvil subjects those findings to adversarial examination and attempts to
expose contradiction, unsupported inference, hidden dependency, authority
expansion, or other failure.

Neither activity, however, has been established as the canonical acceptance
event.

This distinction exposed an unresolved governance problem.

A finding may survive Forge investigation and Anvil verification while still
remaining outside the Accepted canonical Discovery corpus.

FD-0015 established a candidate basis for bounded Originating Procedural
Authority.

FD-0016 established a candidate basis by which Canonical Method Stewardship
Authority may exist as a continuing expression of that bounded authority
where legitimate origin, identity, scope, and provenance remain continuous.

That authority relationship makes governance of the Method's canonical
knowledge corpus a possible act of canonical stewardship.

It does not, however, permit arbitrary findings to enter that corpus.

The remaining question is therefore:

> What must be true for a Forge Discovery to be legitimately accepted into
> the canonical Discovery corpus?

---

# Discovery

Canonical Discovery Acceptance is a governed knowledge-state transition
requiring both sufficient epistemic eligibility and an authorized canonical
commit.

Neither condition is independently sufficient.

Therefore:

    Epistemic Eligibility
            +
    Authorized Canonical Commit
            ↓
    Valid Discovery Acceptance

A Discovery cannot be validly accepted merely because an authorized actor
wishes to accept it.

Likewise, a Discovery does not become canonically Accepted merely because its
finding has survived epistemic investigation and verification.

Canonical acceptance requires both.

---

# Dual Requirement

The two requirements answer different questions.

## Epistemic Eligibility

Epistemic Eligibility concerns whether the candidate finding has satisfied
the applicable engineering and methodological predicates required before it
may legitimately be considered for canonical acceptance.

This concerns the finding.

It does not itself alter canonical state.

## Authorized Canonical Commit

Authorized Canonical Commit concerns whether the governed transition by which
an eligible Discovery enters the canonical Discovery corpus is performed
under the required bounded Procedural Authority.

This concerns the canonical state of the Method.

It does not create the engineering truth of the finding.

Therefore:

    Epistemic Eligibility
            ≠
    Procedural Authority

and:

    Procedural Authority
            ≠
    Epistemic Eligibility

Both are required for valid canonical acceptance.

---

# Forge and Anvil

Forge investigation and Anvil verification may contribute to or establish
epistemic predicates required for Discovery acceptance.

Their occurrence does not, merely by itself, constitute canonical acceptance.

Therefore:

    Forge Discovery
            ≠
    Canonical Acceptance

and:

    Anvil Survival
            ≠
    Canonical Acceptance

The Forge may discover a finding.

The Anvil may fail to falsify that finding.

The artifact may nevertheless remain in a non-Accepted lifecycle state until
an authorized canonical acceptance transition occurs.

This preserves the distinction between verification and commit.

---

# Eligibility Does Not Create Authority

A Discovery may satisfy every applicable epistemic requirement and still lack
a valid acceptance event.

Therefore:

    Eligible
        +
    Unauthorized Commit
        =
    No Valid Acceptance

Epistemic strength cannot substitute for Procedural Authority.

A finding does not acquire authority to alter the canonical corpus merely by
being well supported.

---

# Authority Does Not Create Eligibility

Likewise, an authorized canonical steward cannot make an ineligible finding
eligible merely by committing it.

Therefore:

    Ineligible
        +
    Authorized Commit
        =
    No Valid Acceptance

Procedural Authority cannot substitute for Engineering Authority or
epistemic sufficiency.

This prevents canonical stewardship from becoming authority to manufacture
truth.

---

# Valid Acceptance

A valid canonical acceptance event therefore requires:

    Eligible
        +
    Authorized
        =
    Valid Acceptance

The acceptance event changes the canonical knowledge state of the Method.

It does not change Reality.

It records that a finding which satisfied the applicable epistemic predicates
was admitted to the canonical Discovery corpus through an authorized
procedural transition.

---

# Canonical Stewardship Relationship

Where governance of the canonical Discovery corpus lies within legitimately
established Canonical Method Stewardship Authority, an authorized canonical
steward may possess the bounded Procedural Authority required to perform a
Discovery acceptance commit.

That authority is limited to the governance of the canonical Method and its
knowledge corpus.

It does not establish:

    authority over Reality,
    authority over engineering truth,
    authority over independent Method users,
    authority over derivative Methods,
    or unrestricted authority to alter the Method.

Canonical stewardship therefore supplies a possible authority basis for the
procedural side of Discovery acceptance without supplying the epistemic side.

---

# Acceptance Is a Lifecycle Transition

Canonical acceptance is not merely a textual edit to a Discovery artifact.

Changing:

    Status: Hold

to:

    Status: Accepted

does not by itself establish that a valid acceptance event occurred.

Acceptance is a governed lifecycle transition whose resulting canonical state
must remain inspectable and internally consistent.

The transition must preserve sufficient evidence to determine:

- what Discovery was accepted,
- that the applicable eligibility predicates were satisfied,
- that the acceptance was performed under legitimate Procedural Authority,
- and that the canonical state was changed through the applicable governed
  procedure.

The specific artifacts, metadata, records, version changes, index mutations,
or other mechanisms required to preserve that evidence belong to the
governing acceptance specification.

---

# Acceptance Does Not Create Truth

Canonical acceptance records a Method governance decision.

It does not make the underlying proposition true.

Therefore:

    Engineering Truth
            ≠
    Canonical Status

and:

    Accepted
            ≠
    Infallible

A legitimately Accepted Discovery may later encounter new evidence,
contradiction, qualification, or falsification.

The procedures governing correction, supersession, withdrawal, rejection, or
other post-acceptance lifecycle transitions require separate governance.

---

# Acceptance Does Not Erase Provenance

The acceptance transition must not erase the Discovery's prior lifecycle.

A candidate Discovery may have passed through states involving investigation,
Hold, correction, Forge examination, Anvil examination, or other governed
activity.

Canonical acceptance must extend that history rather than rewrite it.

Therefore:

    Acceptance
        =
    Extension of Canonical History

not:

    Acceptance
        =
    Replacement of Canonical History

The record of how the Discovery reached acceptance remains part of its
inspectable provenance.

---

# Authority Must Be Inspectable

A canonical artifact displaying an Accepted status does not, by that status
alone, prove that an authorized acceptance event occurred.

The acceptance provenance must preserve sufficient evidence to establish the
authority under which the transition was performed.

Therefore:

    Accepted Label
            ≠
    Evidence of Authorized Acceptance

The canonical record must make the legitimacy of the transition inspectable.

The specific mechanism for recording that authority remains a specification
question.

---

# Acceptance Cannot Bootstrap Its Own Authority

Neither this Discovery nor any other Discovery creates the authority required
for its own acceptance merely by asserting that such authority exists.

The authority relationship must be independently established.

Therefore:

    Discovery Assertion of Authority
            ≠
    Creation of Authority

FD-0015, FD-0016, and this Discovery describe candidate authority and
governance relationships.

They do not create those relationships merely by being written, verified, or
Accepted.

Any exercise of acceptance authority must remain grounded in the legitimate
authority relationship that exists independently of the artifact describing
it.

---

# Dependency Status and Epistemic Use

A Discovery may depend epistemically upon a finding memorialized in another
Discovery whose canonical lifecycle remains incomplete.

Therefore:

    Epistemic Dependency
            ≠
    Lifecycle-Status Dependency

A Discovery being on Hold does not, by itself, establish that its finding is
false or unusable for further investigation.

However, reliance upon such a finding does not silently promote the depended-
upon Discovery or bypass its own acceptance lifecycle.

This distinction permits dependency investigation to continue without making
canonical acceptance self-bootstrapping.

---

# Acceptance Procedure

This Discovery establishes the conceptual requirements for valid canonical
Discovery acceptance.

It does not establish the complete operational procedure.

A governing Discovery acceptance specification must separately determine,
as applicable:

- the lifecycle states from which acceptance may occur,
- the required epistemic predicates,
- the required Forge examination,
- the required Anvil examination,
- the authority permitted to perform the transition,
- required acceptance evidence,
- required artifact mutations,
- required index or registry mutations,
- applicable version transitions,
- interruption and failure behavior,
- atomicity or consistency requirements,
- and post-acceptance lifecycle behavior.

Those are procedural implementation questions derived from this Discovery.

They are not established merely by this artifact.

---

# Consequence

Canonical Discovery Acceptance can be separated into two independently
necessary dimensions:

    Epistemic Dimension
        Is the finding eligible?

    Procedural Dimension
        Is the canonical commit authorized?

A valid acceptance event exists only where both dimensions are satisfied.

This preserves the separation between Engineering Authority and Procedural
Authority while allowing legitimately established Canonical Method
Stewardship Authority to govern admission to the Method's canonical Discovery
corpus.

The resulting relationship is:

    Engineering / Methodological Investigation
                    │
                    ▼
          Epistemic Eligibility
                    │
                    │
                    ├──────────────┐
                    │              │
                    │              ▼
                    │     Canonical Method
                    │     Stewardship Authority
                    │              │
                    │              ▼
                    │     Authorized Canonical
                    │            Commit
                    │              │
                    └──────┬───────┘
                           ▼
                 Valid Discovery
                    Acceptance

Neither branch may substitute for the other.

---

# Does Not Establish

This discovery does not establish:

- that Forge investigation alone constitutes acceptance,
- that Anvil survival alone constitutes acceptance,
- that an authorized steward may accept an ineligible finding,
- that epistemic eligibility creates Procedural Authority,
- that Procedural Authority creates engineering truth,
- that an Accepted Discovery is infallible,
- that every Discovery currently on Hold is eligible for acceptance,
- that every previously Accepted Discovery satisfied the procedure eventually
  established from this discovery,
- that canonical stewardship constitutes authority over independent users,
- that canonical stewardship constitutes authority over derivative Methods,
- permanent personal sovereignty over the Method,
- a permanent identity for the authorized canonical steward,
- the exact Discovery acceptance lifecycle,
- the exact version transition required for acceptance,
- the exact acceptance evidence artifact,
- the exact index or registry mutation required,
- post-acceptance correction or supersession procedure,
- or that this Discovery is itself Accepted.
