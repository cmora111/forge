# FD-0019 — Epistemic Eligibility Establishment

**Status:** Hold
**Version:** 0.1.0

---

# Context

FD-0017 established that valid Canonical Discovery Acceptance requires both
sufficient epistemic eligibility and an authorized canonical commit.

FD-0018 established the conditions required for a Forge Discovery to be
epistemically eligible for canonical acceptance.

Those conditions include:

    Applicable Epistemic Examination
                +
    Sufficient Material Dependency Support
                +
    No Known Unresolved Material Defeating Defect
                +
    Sufficient Inspectable Epistemic Evidence
                +
    Evaluated-State Identity
                ↓
        Epistemic Eligibility

FD-0018 also established that epistemic eligibility must be demonstrable.

It deliberately did not establish the actor, office, mechanism, or process
that determines eligibility.

That omission exposed a dependency for the Canonical Discovery Acceptance
Specification.

Before that specification can require epistemic eligibility as a precondition
for canonical acceptance, the Method must distinguish between:

    eligibility that obtains

and:

    a declaration that eligibility obtains.

If epistemic eligibility can be created merely by declaration, then the actor,
office, mechanism, or process making that declaration acquires authority to
manufacture epistemic standing.

If Procedural Authority can make that declaration sufficient merely by virtue
of possessing authority over the canonical corpus, then Procedural Authority
collapses into epistemic judgment.

The remaining question is therefore:

> By what legitimate mechanism may the epistemic eligibility of a Forge
> Discovery be established for purposes of canonical acceptance without
> collapsing epistemic judgment into Procedural Authority?

---

# Discovery

The epistemic eligibility of a Forge Discovery is legitimately established
when an inspectable demonstration shows that the applicable governed
epistemic predicates are satisfied by sufficient evidence for the evaluated
state of the finding.

Eligibility is not created by the authority, discretion, status, identity, or
mere declaration of the actor, office, mechanism, or process making or
recording that determination.

Therefore:

    Governed Epistemic Predicates
                +
    Sufficient Supporting Evidence
                +
    Inspectable Demonstration
                +
    Evaluated-State Correspondence
                ↓
    Epistemic Eligibility Established

The legitimacy of the eligibility determination derives from the demonstrated
relationship between the applicable epistemic predicates, the supporting
evidence, and the evaluated state of the finding.

It does not derive merely from who or what declares the result.

---

# Eligibility Is Not Bestowed

Epistemic eligibility is not a status that becomes epistemically valid merely
because an authorized person, office, mechanism, or process confers it.

Where the applicable epistemic conditions obtain, a declaration does not
create those conditions.

Where the applicable epistemic conditions do not obtain, a declaration cannot
make them obtain.

Therefore:

    Eligibility Declaration
            ≠
    Creation of Epistemic Eligibility

and:

    Authority to Declare
            ≠
    Authority to Manufacture Epistemic Sufficiency

This preserves the distinction between the existence of an epistemic
condition and the recording or recognition of that condition.

---

# Eligibility and Eligibility Determination

Epistemic eligibility and an eligibility determination are distinct.

Epistemic eligibility concerns whether the applicable epistemic conditions
actually obtain for the evaluated finding.

An eligibility determination concerns whether the available evidence
demonstrates that those conditions obtain.

Therefore:

    Epistemic Eligibility
            ≠
    Eligibility Determination

A determination may record, recognize, or conclude that eligibility has been
demonstrated.

The determination does not create the underlying epistemic condition merely
by being issued.

This distinction permits an eligibility determination itself to remain
inspectable, challengeable, and fallible.

---

# Governed Epistemic Predicates

An eligibility determination cannot legitimately rest upon whatever criteria
an evaluator happens to prefer.

The predicates whose satisfaction establishes eligibility must be governed.

At minimum, those predicates must remain consistent with the epistemic
conditions established by FD-0018.

Therefore:

    Evaluator Preference
            ≠
    Governed Epistemic Predicate

and:

    Ad Hoc Threshold
            ≠
    Legitimate Eligibility Basis

The exact predicates, their classification, their applicability to particular
Discovery classes, and the procedure by which they are governed remain
specification and governance questions.

This Discovery establishes that the predicates cannot acquire legitimacy
merely from discretionary use during an eligibility determination.

---

# Sufficient Supporting Evidence

A predicate cannot be treated as satisfied merely because its satisfaction is
asserted.

Evidence sufficient for the predicate and the use made of it must support the
determination.

Therefore:

    Predicate Assertion
            ≠
    Predicate Satisfaction Demonstrated

The supporting evidence may include evidence produced through Forge
investigation, Anvil examination, dependency investigation, defect
disposition, evaluated-state comparison, or other applicable epistemic work.

No evidence source becomes independently sufficient merely because it exists.

The sufficiency of the evidence must remain tied to the predicate it is being
used to demonstrate.

---

# Inspectable Demonstration

Evidence alone does not establish which conclusion the evidence is claimed to
support.

A legitimate eligibility determination requires an inspectable relationship
between:

    the applicable predicate,
    the evidence relied upon,
    and the conclusion that the predicate is satisfied.

Therefore:

    Evidence Exists
            ≠
    Eligibility Demonstrated

The Method must be able to inspect, as applicable:

- which epistemic predicates were applied,
- why those predicates were applicable,
- what evidence was relied upon,
- how that evidence supports the predicate,
- what material dependencies were relied upon,
- what known material defects were considered,
- how unresolved issues were treated,
- what evaluated state of the finding was examined,
- and how the resulting eligibility conclusion was reached.

The exact artifact structure used to preserve that demonstration remains a
specification question.

---

# Evaluated-State Correspondence

An eligibility determination applies to the state of the finding for which
the supporting evidence was evaluated.

A materially different state cannot silently inherit the determination merely
because it retains the same Discovery identifier or artifact location.

Therefore:

    Eligibility Determination for State A
                ≠
    Eligibility Determination for
    Materially Different State B

unless applicable governance establishes that the existing evidence and
determination remain sufficient for the altered state.

This preserves FD-0018's requirement for Evaluated-State Identity.

The exact mechanism by which evaluated-state correspondence is recorded or
verified remains a specification question.

---

# Structural Verification Is Not Epistemic Sufficiency

A mechanism may be capable of verifying structural facts such as:

    required record exists,
    required field is present,
    required examination is recorded,
    required identifier matches.

Those checks may be necessary.

They do not necessarily establish that the evidence contained in those records
is epistemically sufficient.

Therefore:

    Structural Completeness
            ≠
    Epistemic Sufficiency

A mechanical or automated mechanism may establish eligibility only to the
extent that the governed epistemic predicates and their evidentiary
requirements make the relevant determination legitimately demonstrable by
that mechanism.

This Discovery does not require that eligibility determination be performed
by either a human or an automated system.

---

# Forge Does Not Confer Eligibility

Forge investigation and examination may produce evidence relevant to
epistemic eligibility.

The Forge may expose unsupported inference, hidden dependency, contradiction,
scope error, ambiguity, overconstraint, or other defects.

It may also reforge a candidate finding in response.

But:

    Forge Conclusion
            ≠
    Eligibility by Declaration

The Forge does not create epistemic eligibility merely by pronouncing a
Discovery ready.

Any eligibility determination must remain grounded in the applicable governed
predicates and supporting evidence.

This prevents the discovery mechanism from acquiring unrestricted authority
to certify its own output.

---

# Anvil Does Not Confer Eligibility

Anvil examination may provide material epistemic evidence concerning the
finding tested.

Survival of applicable Anvil examination may therefore contribute strongly to
an eligibility demonstration.

But:

    Anvil Survival
            ≠
    Eligibility by Declaration

The Anvil does not independently establish every epistemic predicate required
by FD-0018 merely by returning a survival result.

The evidentiary significance of Anvil examination remains tied to:

- what was tested,
- what state was tested,
- what attacks were applicable,
- what survived,
- and what other epistemic predicates remain to be demonstrated.

This prevents verification success from silently becoming authority to confer
epistemic eligibility.

---

# Procedural Authority Does Not Establish Epistemic Sufficiency

Procedural Authority may legitimately require that epistemic eligibility be
demonstrated before a canonical acceptance transition is permitted.

It may enforce the existence of the required eligibility gate.

It does not thereby acquire authority to manufacture the epistemic condition
required by that gate.

Therefore:

    Procedural Authority
            ≠
    Epistemic Sufficiency

and:

    Authority to Perform Acceptance
            ≠
    Authority to Make an Ineligible Finding Eligible

A canonical acceptance mechanism may refuse to proceed when the required
eligibility demonstration is absent or insufficient.

That refusal does not require Procedural Authority to become the source of
epistemic eligibility.

---

# Determination Must Remain Challengeable

An eligibility determination is not rendered infallible merely because it was
performed under a governed mechanism.

The determination must remain inspectable against:

    the predicates applied,
    the evidence relied upon,
    the evaluated state,
    and the conclusion reached.

A later investigation may expose:

- omitted evidence,
- unsupported dependency,
- misapplied predicate,
- incorrect materiality judgment,
- evaluated-state mismatch,
- contradiction,
- or other defect in the eligibility determination.

Therefore:

    Governed Determination
            ≠
    Infallible Determination

The ability to challenge the determination does not make the determination
meaningless.

It preserves the Method's ability to distinguish justified epistemic standing
from unquestionable declaration.

---

# Disagreement Does Not Create Authority

Different investigators or mechanisms may reach different conclusions from
the same or overlapping evidence.

Disagreement does not grant either conclusion epistemic authority merely
because it was asserted.

Where disagreement concerns a material issue necessary to establish
eligibility, the disagreement must remain visible and subject to the
applicable epistemic governance.

Therefore:

    Conflicting Assertions
            ≠
    Eligibility Resolved

The Method must not hide material epistemic disagreement behind procedural
authority or canonical status.

The exact procedure for resolving such disagreement remains a governance
question.

---

# Eligibility Not Demonstrated Is Not Ineligibility Demonstrated

Failure to demonstrate epistemic eligibility does not itself establish that
the Discovery is epistemically ineligible.

For example, eligibility may fail to be demonstrated because:

- required evidence is missing,
- evidence is incomplete,
- a predicate has not yet been evaluated,
- a material dependency remains unresolved,
- evaluated-state correspondence cannot yet be established,
- or a required determination remains incomplete.

Those conditions may legitimately prevent canonical acceptance from
proceeding.

They do not necessarily establish that the underlying finding is false or
epistemically defective.

Therefore:

    Eligibility Not Demonstrated
                ≠
    Ineligibility Demonstrated

This distinction prevents an incomplete epistemic case from being treated as
a negative epistemic judgment without sufficient basis.

---

# Eligibility Case

The epistemic basis for an eligibility determination must be capable of being
represented as an inspectable case connecting:

    Candidate Discovery
            │
            ▼
    Governed Epistemic Predicates
            │
            ▼
    Supporting Evidence
            │
            ▼
    Demonstrated Satisfaction
            │
            ▼
    Evaluated-State Correspondence
            │
            ▼
    Eligibility Established

This Discovery uses the term **Eligibility Case** to describe that logical
relationship.

It does not require a particular file, document, database record, manifest,
ledger entry, or other storage representation named `Eligibility Case`.

The physical representation of the case remains a specification question.

---

# Relationship to Canonical Acceptance

An established eligibility case answers an epistemic question:

> Has sufficient inspectable evidence demonstrated that the applicable
> epistemic predicates are satisfied for the evaluated state of this
> Discovery?

It does not answer the procedural question:

> Is the canonical acceptance transition authorized and validly performed?

Therefore:

    Eligibility Established
            ≠
    Canonical Acceptance

and:

    Eligibility Established
            ≠
    Procedural Authority

The canonical acceptance mechanism may rely upon an established eligibility
case as a required epistemic precondition.

It must not treat its own Procedural Authority as a substitute for that case.

---

# Consequence

Epistemic eligibility establishment is an evidence-grounded demonstration,
not a discretionary conferral.

The relationship is:

    Applicable Governed
    Epistemic Predicates
            │
            ▼
    Sufficient Supporting Evidence
            │
            ▼
    Inspectable Demonstration
            │
            ▼
    Evaluated-State Correspondence
            │
            ▼
    Eligibility Established
            │
            ▼
    Eligible for Presentation to
    Canonical Acceptance Procedure

The acceptance procedure may require and verify the presence of that
demonstration.

It does not create the epistemic sufficiency that the demonstration records.

---

# Does Not Establish

This discovery does not establish:

- that epistemic eligibility is created by declaration,
- that eligibility determination and epistemic eligibility are identical,
- that an evaluator's identity establishes epistemic sufficiency,
- that a canonical steward may manufacture epistemic eligibility,
- that Procedural Authority creates epistemic sufficiency,
- that Forge examination alone establishes eligibility,
- that an Anvil survival result alone establishes eligibility,
- that structural completeness establishes epistemic sufficiency,
- that every eligibility determination must be performed by a human,
- that every eligibility determination must be performed automatically,
- a new unrestricted epistemic authority,
- that an eligibility determination is infallible,
- that disagreement automatically establishes ineligibility,
- that failure to demonstrate eligibility demonstrates ineligibility,
- the exact governed epistemic predicates for every Discovery class,
- the exact evidence threshold for every epistemic predicate,
- the complete definition of materiality,
- the procedure for resolving material epistemic disagreement,
- the exact actor, office, mechanism, or process that performs an eligibility
  determination,
- the exact artifact or storage representation of an Eligibility Case,
- the canonical Discovery acceptance procedure,
- that eligibility establishment constitutes canonical acceptance,
- that eligibility establishment supplies Procedural Authority,
- or that this Discovery is itself epistemically eligible or Accepted.
