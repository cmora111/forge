# FD-0020 — Procedural Authority Legitimacy

**Status:** Hold
**Version:** 0.1.0

---

# Context

FD-0005 established Dual Authority.

FD-0017 established that valid Canonical Discovery Acceptance requires both
sufficient epistemic eligibility and an authorized canonical commit.

FD-0018 established the conditions required for a Forge Discovery to be
epistemically eligible for canonical acceptance.

FD-0019 established that epistemic eligibility is legitimately established
through an inspectable demonstration that governed epistemic predicates are
satisfied by sufficient supporting evidence for the evaluated state of the
finding, rather than through discretionary conferral.

Those discoveries establish the epistemic side of Canonical Discovery
Acceptance.

They do not establish what makes the Procedural Authority required for the
canonical acceptance transition legitimate.

The Canonical Discovery Acceptance Specification therefore reached a point
where it could require:

    Epistemic Eligibility Established
                +
    Authorized Canonical Commit
                ↓
    Valid Canonical Acceptance

but could not legitimately specify what makes the canonical commit
authorized.

Technical capability cannot answer that question.

Authorship cannot answer it.

Expertise cannot answer it.

Possession of a procedural role cannot answer it merely by assertion.

A rule cannot make its own authority legitimate merely by declaring that
authority.

The remaining question is therefore:

> What gives an actor, office, mechanism, or process legitimate Procedural
> Authority to perform the canonical acceptance transition of a Forge
> Discovery?

---

# Discovery

Derived Procedural Authority to perform a canonical Discovery transition is
legitimately grounded when the actor, office, mechanism, or process exercising
that authority possesses a valid, currently effective, and applicable grant
of authority traceable through legitimate governance to a constitutive
authority source for the canonical domain.

That traceability establishes the governance basis of derived Procedural
Authority.

It does not by itself establish the legitimacy of the constitutive authority
source from which that governance ultimately originates.

Therefore:

    Constitutive Authority Source
                │
                ▼
        Constituted Governance
                │
                ▼
        Valid Authority Grant
                │
                ▼
          Applicable Scope
                │
                ▼
           Current Effect
                │
                ▼
    Actor / Office / Mechanism / Process
                │
                ▼
    Legitimate Procedural Authority
    for the Particular Action

The legitimacy of Procedural Authority does not arise merely from the
capability, identity, authorship, expertise, ownership, role title, or
self-declaration of the entity exercising it.

The authority must possess a legitimate governance basis.

---

# Capability Is Not Authority

An actor, office, mechanism, or process may possess the technical capability
to alter canonical state without possessing legitimate Procedural Authority
to do so.

For example, possession of:

    filesystem write access,
    repository write access,
    administrative access,
    merge capability,
    execution capability,
    or direct control over an artifact

may make a canonical change technically possible.

It does not by itself make that change procedurally legitimate.

Therefore:

    Technical Capability
            ≠
    Procedural Authority

and:

    Ability to Perform an Action
            ≠
    Authorization to Perform that Action

A Method that treats capability as authority cannot distinguish a legitimate
canonical transition from an unauthorized alteration performed by an entity
with sufficient technical access.

---

# Ownership Is Not Sufficient Authority

Ownership or control of the physical or technical resources containing the
canonical corpus may provide extensive practical capability.

It does not by itself establish that every action available to the owner is a
legitimate Stage Method transition.

Therefore:

    Resource Ownership
            ≠
    Unlimited Methodological Procedural Authority

Ownership may participate in the constitution of governance.

It does not eliminate the distinction between control of a resource and
legitimate exercise of a governed canonical transition.

---

# Authorship Is Not Authority

Creating, discovering, investigating, or memorializing a Forge Discovery does
not automatically grant authority to alter its canonical lifecycle state.

Therefore:

    Discovery Authorship
            ≠
    Canonical Acceptance Authority

and:

    Engineering Authority
            ≠
    Procedural Authority

An investigator may possess strong epistemic standing concerning a finding
while possessing no legitimate authority to perform the canonical transition
that admits that finding into the canonical Discovery corpus.

This preserves Dual Authority.

---

# Expertise Is Not Procedural Authority

Expertise may contribute materially to epistemic evaluation.

It does not by itself authorize alteration of canonical state.

Therefore:

    Epistemic Competence
            ≠
    Procedural Authority

An expert may be highly qualified to determine whether evidence supports a
finding while remaining procedurally unauthorized to perform canonical
acceptance.

Conversely, an entity may possess legitimate Procedural Authority while being
prohibited from substituting that authority for the required epistemic
demonstration.

---

# Role Title Is Not Sufficient Authority

A role title does not become legitimate merely because it is written into a
document or assigned informally.

For example:

    Canonical Steward
    Maintainer
    Approver
    Administrator
    Reviewer

does not itself establish the authority associated with the title.

Therefore:

    Role Assertion
            ≠
    Authority Establishment

A role may exercise Procedural Authority only where the authority associated
with that role is validly grounded in the applicable governance.

---

# A Rule Does Not Legitimize Itself

A procedural rule may express, constrain, assign, or delegate authority.

The existence of a rule does not make the rule's authority legitimate merely
because the rule claims that authority.

Otherwise:

    Rule declares authority
            ↓
    Rule becomes authoritative
            ↓
    Authority exists because
    authority was asserted

would allow procedural rules to manufacture their own legitimacy.

Therefore:

    Rule Says Authorized
            ≠
    Rule Is Legitimately Authorized

The legitimacy of a procedural rule's authority must be traceable to a
governance source capable of legitimately establishing that rule.

---

# Derived Authority Requires a Governance Basis

Legitimate derived Procedural Authority must possess a governance basis.

An authority grant cannot become legitimate merely because the recipient
accepts it or successfully exercises it.

The grant must be traceable through the applicable governance structure to a
source capable of constituting or delegating authority for the canonical
domain.

Therefore:

    Authority Assertion
            ≠
    Authority Establishment

and:

    Successful Exercise
            ≠
    Proof of Legitimate Authority

The exact governance structure through which authority is constituted,
assigned, delegated, constrained, revoked, or transferred remains a
governance and specification question.

---

# Constitutive Authority

A governance system cannot require every authority within that system to
derive from an earlier authority within the same system without producing an
unbounded regress.

Therefore the internal authority chain must terminate in a constitutive
authority source for the governed canonical domain.

Constitutive authority differs from derived authority.

Constitutive authority establishes the governance basis from which internal
Procedural Authority may subsequently be derived.

Derived authority receives its legitimacy through that governance basis.

Therefore:

    Constitutive Authority
            │
            ▼
    Governance Established
            │
            ▼
      Derived Authority
            │
            ▼
      Governed Action

The constitutive authority source does not derive its authority from an
earlier internal authority of the governance system it constitutes.

This Discovery does not establish the identity of the constitutive authority
source for the Stage Method or for any particular implementation of it.

Nor does this Discovery establish what makes a constitutive authority source
legitimate in its claim to constitute governance for a particular canonical
domain.

Termination of the internal authority regress identifies the required
governance root.

It does not by itself justify that root.

---

# Derived Authority

Procedural Authority exercised within an established governance system may be
derived through a valid grant, assignment, delegation, or other governed
authorization mechanism.

Derived authority remains dependent upon the authority from which it was
legitimately derived.

Therefore:

    Derived Authority
            ≠
    Self-Originating Authority

An entity receiving derived authority does not acquire greater authority than
the applicable governance permits the granting source to convey.

The exact forms of delegation, assignment, inheritance, or transfer remain
governance questions.

---

# Authority Must Be Scoped

A valid authority grant does not imply unrestricted authority.

The grant must apply to the action being performed.

Therefore:

    Authority for Action A
            ≠
    Authority for Every Action

An actor, office, mechanism, or process authorized to perform Canonical
Discovery Acceptance does not thereby acquire authority to:

- alter unrelated canonical states,
- rewrite constitutional governance,
- destroy epistemic evidence,
- bypass required eligibility conditions,
- alter authority grants outside its scope,
- or perform other actions not included within its legitimate authority.

The exact scope of a particular authority grant remains a governance question.

---

# Authority Must Be Currently Effective

An authority grant may have been legitimate when created and still be
inapplicable at the time an action is performed.

Authority may, depending upon applicable governance, be:

- revoked,
- suspended,
- expired,
- superseded,
- limited by condition,
- or otherwise rendered ineffective.

Therefore:

    Historically Valid Authority
            ≠
    Necessarily Current Authority

For a particular exercise of Procedural Authority to be legitimate, the
applicable authority must be effective at the time of the governed action.

The exact mechanisms for activation, expiration, suspension, revocation, or
supersession remain governance questions.

---

# Authority Must Be Applicable

An authority grant must apply to the canonical domain, transition, object, or
other governed subject upon which the authority is exercised.

Therefore:

    Valid Authority Elsewhere
            ≠
    Valid Authority Here

An entity may possess legitimate authority within one domain while possessing
no authority over another.

Likewise, authority over one class of canonical transition does not
automatically establish authority over every canonical transition.

Applicability must therefore remain demonstrable for the particular governed
action.

---

# Authority Basis Must Be Inspectable

A claim of Procedural Authority must be capable of being traced to its
governance basis.

Otherwise the Method cannot distinguish:

    legitimate authority

from:

    asserted authority

or:

    technical capability.

The authority basis must therefore be inspectable enough to establish, as
applicable:

- the authority being exercised,
- the entity exercising it,
- the scope of that authority,
- the applicability of that scope to the action,
- the current effectiveness of the authority,
- the grant, assignment, delegation, or constitutive basis from which it
  derives,
- and the governance source supporting that basis.

Therefore:

    Authority Claim
            ≠
    Authority Demonstrated

The exact artifact or representation used to preserve the authority basis
remains a specification question.

---

# Authority May Be Exercised by More Than a Human Actor

Procedural Authority is not necessarily limited to a human individual.

Applicable governance may authorize:

- a person,
- an office,
- a role,
- a governed process,
- an automated mechanism,
- or another explicitly governed entity

to perform a canonical action.

Therefore:

    Procedural Authority
            ≠
    Human Authority Only

A non-human mechanism does not acquire authority merely because it can
execute the transition.

Its authority must satisfy the same requirements of legitimate governance
basis, scope, applicability, and current effect.

---

# Possession of Authority Is Not Valid Exercise

Possession of legitimate Procedural Authority does not by itself make every
action performed by the authorized entity valid.

An authorized entity may act:

- outside its authority scope,
- after its authority has ceased to be effective,
- upon an object outside its jurisdiction,
- without satisfying required procedural preconditions,
- or in violation of governing transition requirements.

Therefore:

    Authorized Entity
            ≠
    Automatically Valid Action

and:

    Legitimate Procedural Authority
                +
    Invalid Exercise
                ≠
    Valid Canonical Transition

A valid canonical transition requires both legitimate authority and
compliance with the governing conditions applicable to the exercise of that
authority.

This prevents authority from swallowing the procedural and epistemic
constraints that authority is required to obey.

---

# Relationship to Epistemic Eligibility

Procedural Authority and epistemic eligibility remain independent dimensions
of valid Canonical Discovery Acceptance.

Therefore:

    Epistemic Eligibility
            ≠
    Procedural Authority

and:

    Procedural Authority
            ≠
    Epistemic Eligibility

An epistemically eligible Discovery cannot become canonically Accepted merely
because its eligibility has been demonstrated.

An entity possessing legitimate Procedural Authority cannot make an
epistemically ineligible or insufficiently demonstrated Discovery eligible
merely by exercising that authority.

The relationship remains:

    Epistemic Eligibility Established
                +
    Legitimate Procedural Authority
                +
    Valid Exercise of that Authority
                ↓
    Candidate Basis for
    Valid Canonical Acceptance

The complete acceptance procedure remains a specification question.

---

# Authority Does Not Validate Its Own Exercise

An entity's possession of Procedural Authority cannot by itself demonstrate
that a particular exercise of that authority was valid.

The Method must remain able to distinguish:

    entity possesses authority

from:

    authority was validly exercised
    in this particular transition.

Therefore:

    Authority Possessed
            ≠
    Exercise Validated

The evidence required to demonstrate a valid exercise remains dependent upon
the applicable governance and transition specification.

This preserves the possibility of identifying invalid actions performed by
otherwise authorized entities.

---

# Consequence

Legitimate derived Procedural Authority is a governed and traceable
relationship, not a consequence of technical capability, authorship,
expertise, ownership, role title, or self-declaration.

Traceability establishes the governance basis of derived Procedural Authority.
It does not by itself establish the legitimacy of the constitutive authority
source at which the internal authority chain terminates.

The relationship is:

    Constitutive Authority Source
                │
                ▼
        Constituted Governance
                │
                ▼
        Valid Authority Grant
                │
                ▼
          Applicable Scope
                │
                ▼
           Current Effect
                │
                ▼
      Authority Demonstrated
                │
                ▼
    Legitimate Procedural Authority
    for the Particular Action

Possession of that authority establishes that the governed entity may perform
the action within the authority granted to it.

It does not establish that every exercise of that authority is valid.

The exercise must still satisfy the governing conditions applicable to the
particular canonical transition.

---

# Does Not Establish

This discovery does not establish:

- the identity of the constitutive authority source for the Stage Method,
- the identity of the constitutive authority source for any particular
  implementation,
- who should perform Canonical Discovery Acceptance,
- that the author of a Discovery possesses acceptance authority,
- that an investigator possesses acceptance authority,
- that a repository owner possesses unlimited Methodological Procedural
  Authority,
- that technical capability establishes Procedural Authority,
- that filesystem or repository access establishes Procedural Authority,
- that expertise establishes Procedural Authority,
- that a role title establishes Procedural Authority,
- that a procedural rule legitimizes itself,
- that successful exercise proves legitimate authority,
- that authority may be self-declared,
- a particular governance constitution,
- a particular authority hierarchy,
- a particular delegation mechanism,
- a particular revocation mechanism,
- a particular suspension mechanism,
- a particular authority-record artifact,
- whether authority may be delegated further,
- how conflicting authority claims are resolved,
- how constitutive authority is selected for a particular implementation,
- that Procedural Authority creates epistemic eligibility,
- that epistemic eligibility creates Procedural Authority,
- that possession of legitimate Procedural Authority makes every exercise
  valid,
- the complete conditions for a valid exercise of Procedural Authority,
- the complete Canonical Discovery Acceptance procedure,
- that an authorized canonical commit alone establishes valid Canonical
  Discovery Acceptance,
- or that this Discovery is itself epistemically eligible or Accepted,
- what makes a constitutive authority source legitimate in its claim to
  constitute governance for a particular canonical domain.
