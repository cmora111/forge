# FD-0021 — Constitutive Authority Standing

**Status:** Hold
**Version:** 0.1.0

---

# Context

FD-0005 established Dual Authority.

FD-0017 established that valid Canonical Discovery Acceptance requires both
sufficient epistemic eligibility and an authorized canonical commit.

FD-0018 established the conditions required for a Forge Discovery to be
epistemically eligible for canonical acceptance.

FD-0019 established how epistemic eligibility is legitimately demonstrated.

FD-0020 established that legitimate derived Procedural Authority requires a
valid, currently effective, and applicable authority grant traceable through
constituted governance to a constitutive authority source for the canonical
domain.

FD-0020 also established an important boundary:

    Termination of an Authority Regress
                    ≠
    Justification of the Authority Root

The internal authority chain may terminate at a constitutive authority source
without thereby establishing that the source possesses legitimate standing to
constitute governance for the domain.

The Canonical Discovery Acceptance Specification therefore reached another
blocking dependency.

It can trace derived Procedural Authority through constituted governance to a
constitutive authority source.

It cannot yet establish what makes that source's constitutive claim
legitimate.

The remaining question is therefore:

> What gives a constitutive authority source legitimate standing to
> constitute governance for a canonical domain?

---

# Discovery

A constitutive authority source does not acquire legitimate standing merely
by declaring itself authoritative, possessing technical control, originating
a resource, owning a resource, being recognized as authoritative, acting
first, or terminating an internal authority regress.

Its constitutive standing must be grounded in a relationship to the canonical
domain sufficient, under the normative framework applicable to that domain,
to support the act of constituting governance.

Therefore:

    Constitutive Authority Source
                │
                ▼
    Grounding Relationship to Domain
                │
                ▼
    Evaluated Under Applicable
       Normative Framework
                │
                ▼
    Constitutive Standing Established
                │
                ▼
      Governance May Be Constituted

The grounding relationship may differ according to the nature and origin of
the canonical domain.

This Discovery does not establish one universal grounding relationship for
all domains.

Nor does it establish the ultimate legitimacy of the normative framework
under which the sufficiency of the grounding relationship is evaluated.

---

# Prior Authorization Is Not Required in Every Case

A constitutive authority source cannot be required in every case to derive
its constitutive standing from an earlier authority within the governance
order it constitutes.

If:

    Authority A
        │
        ▼
    authorizes Authority B
        │
        ▼
    Authority B constitutes governance

then Authority B possesses derived authority with respect to Authority A.

Requiring every constitutive authority source to obtain authorization from an
earlier internal authority would reproduce the authority regress that
constitutive authority exists to terminate.

Therefore:

    Constitutive Standing
            ≠
    Necessarily Prior Internal Authorization

This does not mean that constitutive authority can never derive standing from
an external or broader governance domain.

---

# Constitutive Authority Is Domain-Relative

An authority may be constitutive with respect to one governance domain while
remaining derived with respect to another.

For example:

    Broader Governance Domain
                │
                ▼
       Derived Authority
                │
                ▼
       Local Domain Source
                │
                ▼
    Constitutive Authority
    relative to Local Domain

Therefore:

    Constitutive
            ≠
    Universally Underived

Constitutive authority identifies the governance root for the domain being
examined.

It does not necessarily identify an authority that is independent of every
broader legal, contractual, organizational, institutional, or other
governance relationship.

---

# Creation Is Not Universally Sufficient

Creating or originating a resource or domain establishes a factual
relationship between the originator and what was created.

It does not establish a universal rule that every creator possesses unlimited
or perpetual constitutive authority over everything that develops from that
creation.

Therefore:

    Creation
        ≠
    Unlimited Constitutive Authority

Origination may form part or all of the grounding relationship supporting
initial constitutive standing in an appropriate domain.

Whether it is sufficient depends upon the nature of the domain and the
normative framework applicable to it.

---

# Ownership Is Not Universally Sufficient

Ownership may provide a relevant relationship between a source and a
canonical domain.

It does not by itself establish that every governance act available to an
owner is legitimate.

Therefore:

    Ownership
        ≠
    Unlimited Constitutive Standing

Ownership may participate in the grounding basis for constitutive standing.

Its significance depends upon the applicable domain and normative framework.

---

# Technical Control Is Not Constitutive Standing

Technical control over a canonical domain does not by itself establish the
right to constitute its governance.

Possession of:

    filesystem control,
    repository administration,
    credentials,
    infrastructure control,
    physical possession,
    or execution capability

may make constitution or reconstitution technically possible.

It does not make the act legitimate.

Therefore:

    Technical Control
            ≠
    Constitutive Standing

A source that obtains technical control without a sufficient grounding
relationship to the domain does not acquire constitutive standing merely
through possession of that capability.

---

# Acting First Is Not Constitutive Standing

The first entity to declare governance does not become legitimate
constitutive authority merely because no competing declaration preceded it.

Otherwise constitutive legitimacy would reduce to priority of assertion.

Therefore:

    First Assertion
            ≠
    Constitutive Standing

A governance claim must possess a sufficient grounding relationship to the
domain.

Chronological priority alone does not supply that relationship.

---

# Recognition Is Not Universally Sufficient

Recognition by participants or other actors may contribute materially to the
standing of a constitutive authority source.

Recognition alone does not universally establish constitutive legitimacy.

Recognition may be:

- mistaken,
- coerced,
- uninformed,
- incomplete,
- unavailable at initial constitution,
- or itself dependent upon an existing governance relationship.

Therefore:

    Recognition
            ≠
    Universally Sufficient
    Constitutive Standing

Recognition may participate in a grounding relationship where the applicable
normative framework gives it such significance.

---

# Consent May Participate in Constitutive Standing

Consent or agreement among governed participants may provide a powerful
grounding relationship for constitutive governance.

However, consent is not a universal explanation for every constitutive act.

A canonical domain may initially exist before additional participants exist.

Future participants may not have participated in its original constitution.

Some governance domains may also possess obligations or constraints that
cannot be displaced merely by participant agreement.

Therefore:

    Consent
        may ground
    Constitutive Standing

but:

    Consent
        ≠
    Universal Source of
    Every Form of Legitimacy

The significance of consent depends upon the applicable normative framework
and the domain being governed.

---

# Participation May Establish a Governance Relationship

Voluntary participation in an already constituted domain may establish or
strengthen the applicability of that domain's governance to the participant.

Therefore:

    Existing Governance
            │
            ▼
    Voluntary Participation
            │
            ▼
    Governance Relationship
    May Become Applicable

This may help explain why governance legitimately applies to a participant.

It does not by itself explain the legitimacy of the original constitutive act
that established the governance before the participant entered the domain.

---

# Necessity Does Not Establish Standing

A canonical domain may require governance in order to function coherently.

That necessity does not identify which source possesses legitimate standing
to constitute that governance.

Therefore:

    Governance Is Necessary
            ≠
    Particular Source Has
    Constitutive Standing

Necessity may explain why a constitutive act is required.

It does not by itself establish who may legitimately perform that act.

---

# A Grounding Relationship Is Required

A constitutive authority claim must possess a grounding relationship to the
canonical domain sufficient to support the act of constitution under the
normative framework applicable to that domain.

An arbitrary entity lacking such a relationship cannot acquire constitutive
standing merely by asserting it.

Therefore:

    Constitutive Claim
            +
    No Sufficient Grounding Relationship
            ↓
    Constitutive Standing
    Not Established

The grounding relationship may arise through different structures depending
upon the domain.

Examples may include:

- origination,
- joint origination,
- agreement,
- externally delegated standing,
- ownership or entrusted control,
- contractual standing,
- organizational standing,
- or another domain-establishing relationship recognized as sufficient under
  the applicable normative framework.

This list is illustrative, not exhaustive.

---

# The Grounding Relationship Bounds the Standing

Constitutive standing cannot legitimately exceed what its grounding
relationship supports.

Therefore:

    Grounding Relationship
            │
            ▼
    Bounds Constitutive Standing

For example:

    Joint Origination
            ≠
    Automatic Unilateral Authority

    External Delegation
            ≠
    Authority Beyond Delegation

    Initial Origination
            ≠
    Necessarily Perpetual Authority

The scope and persistence of constitutive standing depend upon the grounding
relationship and the normative framework applicable to it.

---

# Initial Constitution and Reconstitution Are Distinct

The initial constitution of governance for a domain without an existing
internal governance order differs from replacing or altering governance in a
domain where governing relationships already exist.

Therefore:

    Initial Constitution
            ≠
    Reconstitution

A source that may legitimately establish initial governance does not
necessarily possess unrestricted standing to replace governance after the
domain has acquired additional governing relationships, rights, obligations,
participants, or constraints.

Reconstitution may therefore require a different or additional grounding
basis.

The exact conditions for legitimate reconstitution remain a governance
question.

---

# Constitutive Standing Does Not Validate Every Constituted Rule

Possession of legitimate constitutive standing establishes that the source
may constitute governance within the bounds of that standing.

It does not establish that every rule, authority grant, constraint, or
governance provision created through that act is legitimate merely because
the constitutive source created it.

Therefore:

    Legitimate Constitutive Standing
                +
    Governance Provision
                ≠
    Automatically Legitimate Provision

This preserves the distinction between:

    legitimate standing to constitute

and:

    legitimate exercise of that standing.

The validity conditions applicable to particular governance provisions remain
dependent upon the applicable normative framework and governance domain.

---

# Descriptive Facts Do Not Alone Establish Normative Standing

Facts about a source may establish relationships to a canonical domain.

For example:

    Source created the domain.
    Source owns the infrastructure.
    Source controls the repository.
    Source wrote the original artifacts.
    Source acted first.

These are descriptive facts.

Constitutive standing is a normative conclusion concerning whether the source
may legitimately establish governance.

Therefore:

    Descriptive Relationship
            ≠
    Automatically Sufficient
    Normative Standing

The significance of a descriptive relationship must be evaluated under the
normative framework applicable to the domain.

This prevents the Method from silently converting factual capability,
possession, chronology, or origination into legitimate authority.

---

# Applicable Normative Framework

Determining whether a grounding relationship is sufficient to establish
constitutive standing requires a normative basis for evaluating that
relationship.

The applicable normative framework may differ according to the domain.

It may arise from, for example:

- a broader governance order,
- legal or contractual relationships,
- organizational governance,
- voluntary association,
- an established constitutional framework,
- founding conditions,
- or another applicable normative order.

This Discovery does not establish which normative framework applies to every
canonical domain.

It establishes that the sufficiency of a constitutive grounding relationship
cannot be determined merely from descriptive facts or authority assertion.

---

# Normative Framework Applicability Must Be Established

A normative framework cannot govern the evaluation of constitutive standing
merely because it is asserted to be applicable.

The basis for applying that framework to the canonical domain must itself be
identifiable.

Therefore:

    Normative Framework Identified
                ≠
    Normative Framework Applicable

The applicability basis may arise from the relationship between the domain
and a broader governance order, legal or contractual relationships,
organizational structure, founding conditions, voluntary adoption, or another
basis capable of connecting the normative framework to the domain.

This Discovery does not establish one universal mechanism by which normative
framework applicability is determined.

It establishes that applicability must be capable of being demonstrated
rather than assumed.

Establishing that a normative framework applies to a domain does not by
itself establish the ultimate legitimacy of that normative framework.

---

# Normative Framework Legitimacy Remains Separate

Identifying an applicable normative framework does not by itself establish
the ultimate legitimacy of that framework.

Therefore:

    Applicable Normative Framework
                ≠
    Ultimately Justified
    Normative Framework

This Discovery establishes the dependency required to evaluate constitutive
standing.

It does not claim to resolve the deeper question of what gives the applicable
normative framework itself legitimate authority to determine which
constitutive relationships are sufficient.

That question remains outside the finding established here unless and until
the engineering process demonstrates that it is a blocking dependency.

---

# Constitutive Standing Must Be Inspectable

A claim of constitutive standing must be capable of inspection.

The Method must be able to identify, as applicable:

- the claimed constitutive authority source,
- the canonical domain being constituted,
- the grounding relationship between source and domain,
- the normative framework under which that relationship is evaluated,
- the basis establishing that the normative framework is applicable to the
  canonical domain,
- the basis for concluding that the relationship is sufficient,
- the scope of the resulting constitutive standing,
- and any conditions or limitations affecting that standing.

Therefore:

    Constitutive Standing Claimed
                ≠
    Constitutive Standing Demonstrated

The exact artifact or representation used to preserve this basis remains a
specification question.

---

# Consequence

A constitutive authority source is not legitimate merely because the internal
authority chain terminates there.

Nor does constitutive legitimacy arise merely from technical control,
ownership, creation, chronological priority, recognition, or assertion.

Instead:

Canonical Domain
        │
        ▼
Source / Domain Relationship
        │
        ▼
Normative Framework Identified
        │
        ▼
Applicability to Domain Established
        │
        ▼
Sufficiency of Relationship
        │
        ▼
Constitutive Standing
        │
        ▼
Governance May Be Constituted

The grounding relationship may differ across domains.

Constitutive authority is therefore domain-relative.

A source may possess constitutive standing within one domain while deriving
its standing from a broader governance domain.

The resulting standing remains bounded by the relationship that supports it
and does not automatically make every exercise of that standing legitimate.

---

# Does Not Establish

This discovery does not establish:

- the identity of the constitutive authority source for the Stage Method,
- the identity of the constitutive authority source for any particular
  implementation,
- one universal source of constitutive legitimacy,
- that creation alone establishes constitutive standing,
- that ownership alone establishes constitutive standing,
- that technical control establishes constitutive standing,
- that acting first establishes constitutive standing,
- that recognition alone establishes constitutive standing,
- that consent is the universal source of constitutive legitimacy,
- that participation alone establishes the legitimacy of an original
  constitutive act,
- that necessity establishes who possesses constitutive standing,
- that constitutive authority must always derive from prior internal
  authorization,
- that constitutive authority is universally underived,
- that origination necessarily establishes perpetual authority,
- that a constitutive authority source possesses unlimited authority,
- that legitimate constitutive standing validates every governance provision,
- that initial constitution and reconstitution have identical legitimacy
  conditions,
- a complete taxonomy of constitutive grounding relationships,
- a particular normative framework for every canonical domain,
- one universal mechanism for establishing the applicability of a normative
  framework to a canonical domain,
- that establishing normative-framework applicability establishes the
  ultimate legitimacy of that normative framework,
- the ultimate legitimacy of any applicable normative framework,
- what gives a normative framework legitimate authority to determine which
  constitutive relationships are sufficient,
- the complete procedure for demonstrating constitutive standing,
- the artifact in which constitutive standing must be recorded,
- the complete governance constitution of the Stage Method,
- the complete Canonical Discovery Acceptance procedure,
- that constitutive standing creates epistemic eligibility,
- that epistemic eligibility creates constitutive standing,
- that this Discovery is itself epistemically eligible,
- or that this Discovery is Accepted.
