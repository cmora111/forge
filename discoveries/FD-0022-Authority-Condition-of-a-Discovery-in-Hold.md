# FD-0022 — Authority Condition of a Discovery in Hold

**Status:** Hold  
**Version:** 0.1.0

---

# Discovery

A Forge Discovery in Hold is a governed proposed Discovery whose finding has
not acquired the canonical standing conferred by Canonical Discovery
Acceptance.

Hold does not confer epistemic authority, normative authority, or canonical
acceptance authority upon the finding of the Discovery.

Therefore:

    Governed Discovery Candidate
                ≠
    Authoritative Finding

and:

    Hold
      ≠
    Canonical Discovery Acceptance

A Discovery may exist as an identifiable, governed subject of investigation
without its finding thereby possessing the authority of an Accepted
Discovery.

---

# Context

During engineering of the Canonical Discovery Acceptance Specification, the
Stage initially modeled Hold and Accepted as two canonical acceptance states.

That model produced a contradiction.

The specification described Canonical Discovery Acceptance as the transition
by which a Discovery is admitted into the canonical Discovery corpus while
also describing a Discovery in Hold as already existing within that corpus.

The Anvil therefore exposed an unresolved distinction between:

    Discovery existence before Acceptance

and:

    Discovery standing after Acceptance

Initial examination suggested that a Discovery in Hold might already possess
canonical identity and authoritative canonical state.

Further Forge examination, informed by the existing Stage Admission model,
showed that this conclusion was not required.

The Stage Method already permits governed proposed artifacts to exist, be
identified, inspected, evaluated, and prepared for a governed transition
without thereby possessing the authority conferred by successful admission.

The same distinction is required for Forge Discoveries.

---

# The Problem

A Forge Discovery must be capable of existing before Canonical Discovery
Acceptance.

Before Acceptance, the Discovery may need to be:

- identified;
- memorialized;
- inspected;
- modified;
- reforged;
- tested on the Anvil;
- associated with evidence;
- evaluated for epistemic eligibility;
- referenced as the subject of an investigation; and
- preserved with an inspectable history.

None of those capabilities, by themselves, establish that the finding of the
Discovery is authoritative.

If merely placing a Discovery in Hold conferred authority upon its finding,
then a finding could acquire governing force before Canonical Discovery
Acceptance.

That would permit:

    Proposition Written
            │
            ▼
    Status: Hold
            │
            ▼
    Finding Authoritative

This would bypass the acceptance mechanism.

Therefore, the authority condition of a Discovery in Hold must be
distinguished from the governed existence of the Discovery artifact and its
investigative role.

---

# Governed Existence Does Not Establish Authority

A Discovery may be governed before its finding is authoritative.

Therefore:

    Governed Existence
            ≠
    Canonical Authority

The existence of procedures governing the identity, preservation,
modification, investigation, or evaluation of a Discovery does not establish
that the finding contained within that Discovery has been canonically
Accepted.

Governance of the candidate and authority of the finding are distinct.

A Discovery in Hold may therefore participate in the governed Forge process
without its finding possessing the standing conferred by Canonical Discovery
Acceptance.

---

# Hold

Hold identifies the pre-Acceptance condition of a governed proposed
Discovery.

A Discovery in Hold has been memorialized as a Discovery candidate and may
participate in the governed investigative process.

Hold may support such activities as:

- continued Forge examination;
- reforge;
- Anvil testing;
- evidence development;
- epistemic evaluation;
- dependency discovery;
- investigative reference; and
- preservation of the Discovery's development history.

Hold does not, merely by being established, confer authority upon the
Discovery's finding.

Therefore:

    Hold
      ≠
    Epistemic Authority

    Hold
      ≠
    Normative Authority

    Hold
      ≠
    Canonical Acceptance Authority

    Hold
      ≠
    Accepted

Hold also does not establish that the Discovery is false, rejected, failed,
or epistemically ineligible.

It establishes that the Discovery remains a governed candidate whose finding
has not undergone Canonical Discovery Acceptance.

---

# Hold Is Not Mere Artifact Existence

The absence of acceptance authority does not make Hold meaningless.

An arbitrary artifact does not become a governed Discovery candidate merely
because it physically exists or contains text resembling a Forge Discovery.

Therefore:

    Artifact Existence
            ≠
    Hold

Hold represents a procedural condition within the governed Discovery process.

The exact mechanism by which a proposed Discovery is memorialized into Hold
is not established by this Discovery.

Accordingly:

    Physical Artifact
            │
            │ governed memorialization
            │ mechanism not established here
            ▼
    Discovery in Hold
            │
            │ Forge / Reforge / Anvil
            │ epistemic investigation
            ▼
    Eligible for Canonical
    Acceptance Evaluation
            │
            │ valid Canonical
            │ Discovery Acceptance
            ▼
    Accepted Discovery

The existence of an unresolved memorialization mechanism does not itself
confer authority upon the finding of a Discovery in Hold.

---

# Investigative Dependency Is Not Authoritative Dependency

A Discovery in Hold may participate in an investigative dependency
relationship.

For example, examination of one Discovery may expose another question,
dependency, contradiction, or required investigation.

The resulting Discovery may identify the earlier Discovery as the source of
that investigative dependency.

This does not establish that the finding of the earlier Hold Discovery is an
authoritative premise.

Therefore:

    Investigative Dependency
            ≠
    Authoritative Dependency

A Hold Discovery may explain why another investigation exists without its
finding being treated as canonically established.

Any use of a Discovery finding as an authoritative dependency must satisfy
the requirements applicable to authoritative Discovery standing.

---

# Epistemic Eligibility Does Not Change Hold by Itself

Epistemic eligibility and Hold describe different conditions.

A Discovery may remain in Hold after epistemic eligibility has been
established.

Therefore:

    Hold
      +
    Epistemically Eligible

is coherent.

Establishing epistemic eligibility does not itself confer Canonical Discovery
Acceptance.

Therefore:

    Epistemic Eligibility Established
                ≠
    Accepted

Likewise, Hold does not establish epistemic ineligibility.

The epistemic condition of the Discovery and the acceptance standing of its
finding remain distinct.

---

# Canonical Discovery Acceptance Changes Standing

Canonical Discovery Acceptance does not create the physical Discovery
artifact.

Nor does it create the investigation that preceded Acceptance.

Instead, Canonical Discovery Acceptance changes the standing of the finding
of an already-governed Discovery candidate.

Before valid Acceptance:

    Discovery in Hold
            │
            ▼
    Governed Candidate
    Finding Not Accepted

After valid Acceptance:

    Accepted Discovery
            │
            ▼
    Finding Possesses the
    Canonical Standing Conferred
    by Acceptance

Therefore:

    Artifact Creation
            ≠
    Canonical Discovery Acceptance

and:

    Governed Candidate Existence
            ≠
    Canonical Discovery Acceptance

Canonical Discovery Acceptance governs the transition by which the finding of
a Discovery in Hold acquires the canonical standing associated with an
Accepted Discovery.

---

# Hold and Accepted Are Not Equivalent Authority Conditions

Hold and Accepted must not be treated as though they confer equivalent
standing upon a Discovery finding.

They lie on opposite sides of the Canonical Discovery Acceptance boundary.

Conceptually:

    Discovery in Hold
            │
            │ governed investigation
            │ no authority conferred
            │ upon finding by Hold
            ▼
    Acceptance Preconditions
    Satisfied
            │
            ▼
    Valid Canonical Discovery
    Acceptance
            │
            ▼
    Accepted Discovery
            │
            ▼
    Canonical Standing
    Conferred by Acceptance

This does not require Hold and Accepted to be represented by different
physical repositories, directories, registries, or storage mechanisms.

The distinction is one of governed standing, not necessarily physical
location.

---

# Canonical Corpus Distinction

The phrase "canonical Discovery corpus" must not be used in a manner that
collapses governed Discovery candidates and Accepted Discoveries into the
same authority condition.

A system may physically or administratively maintain proposed and Accepted
Discoveries together.

Such common custody does not establish equivalent standing.

Therefore:

    Common Storage
            ≠
    Common Authority

and:

    Governed Custody
            ≠
    Canonical Acceptance

The term "canonical Discovery corpus" MUST NOT, by itself, be used to infer
that every Discovery maintained within the structure possesses the standing
conferred by Canonical Discovery Acceptance.

Where a structure contains both governed Discovery candidates and Accepted
Discoveries, that structure must preserve the distinction between candidate
standing and Accepted Discovery standing.

This Discovery does not establish whether a Discovery in Hold is considered
a member of, outside of, or otherwise associated with a structure designated
as the canonical Discovery corpus.

---

# Inspectability

The authority condition of a Discovery MUST be inspectable.

For a Discovery in Hold, it must be possible to determine that:

- the Discovery is a governed candidate;
- the Discovery has not undergone Canonical Discovery Acceptance;
- Hold itself does not confer authority upon the finding;
- any epistemic eligibility determination is distinct from Acceptance; and
- any dependency upon the Discovery distinguishes investigative use from
  authoritative reliance.

The mere presence of the Discovery within a governed repository, directory,
registry, index, or other structure MUST NOT be treated as sufficient evidence
that its finding is Accepted.

---

# Consequence

The Discovery lifecycle relevant to Canonical Discovery Acceptance must
preserve the distinction:

    Artifact May Exist
            │
            ▼
    Governed Discovery Candidate
            │
            ▼
          Hold
            │
            │ Forge / Reforge / Anvil
            │ epistemic evaluation
            ▼
    Acceptance Preconditions
    May Become Satisfied
            │
            ▼
    Valid Canonical Discovery
    Acceptance
            │
            ▼
        Accepted
            │
            ▼
    Canonical Standing of Finding

Therefore, engineering that governs Canonical Discovery Acceptance must not
assume that Hold already confers the canonical standing that Acceptance is
responsible for establishing.

Any specification that models Hold and Accepted as peer states carrying the
same kind of canonical authority must be examined for this collapse.

---

# Does Not Establish

This Discovery does not establish:

- the complete lifecycle of a Forge Discovery;
- the exact mechanism by which a proposed Discovery is memorialized into
  Hold;
- the actor or authority permitted to memorialize a Discovery into Hold;
- the exact artifact format required for a Discovery in Hold;
- a requirement that Hold and Accepted Discoveries occupy different physical
  repositories, directories, registries, or storage systems;
- the complete semantics of every possible Discovery status;
- the method by which epistemic eligibility is established;
- the requirements for Canonical Discovery Acceptance;
- the exact canonical standing conferred upon an Accepted Discovery beyond
  the fact that Hold does not already confer that standing;
- whether an Accepted Discovery may later be withdrawn, superseded,
  deprecated, invalidated, or otherwise transitioned;
- the complete rules governing authoritative dependency upon Accepted
  Discoveries;
- the ultimate epistemic truth of a Discovery merely because it is Accepted;
  or
- that this Discovery is Accepted.

---

# Open Dependencies

This Discovery exposes but does not resolve the following engineering
questions:

1. What governed mechanism establishes that a proposed Discovery has entered
   Hold?

2. What authority is required to perform that memorialization?

3. What exact standing is conferred upon a Discovery finding by Canonical
   Discovery Acceptance?

4. How must the canonical Discovery corpus represent or distinguish governed
   candidates from Accepted Discoveries?

These questions do not alter the central finding that Hold itself does not
confer the authority of an Accepted Discovery.

They become blocking dependencies only where subsequent engineering
demonstrates that their resolution is required.

# Version History

## 0.1.0 — Hold

Initial memorialization.

Established the candidate finding that Hold identifies a governed proposed
Forge Discovery without conferring epistemic, normative, or canonical
acceptance authority upon its finding.

Distinguished governed candidate existence from the canonical standing
conferred by Canonical Discovery Acceptance.

Established that investigative dependency upon a Hold Discovery does not
constitute authoritative dependency upon its finding.

Identified the prior assumption that Hold and Accepted are equivalent
canonical authority conditions as requiring re-examination.

Recorded unresolved questions concerning memorialization into Hold, the
authority required for that act, the exact standing conferred by Acceptance,
and representation of proposed and Accepted Discoveries within the Discovery
system.
