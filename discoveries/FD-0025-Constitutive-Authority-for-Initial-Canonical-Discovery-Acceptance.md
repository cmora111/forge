# FD-0025 — Constitutive Authority for Initial Canonical Discovery Acceptance

**Status:** Hold
**Version:** 0.1.1

---

# Context

FD-0017 established that valid Canonical Discovery Acceptance requires both
sufficient epistemic eligibility and an authorized canonical commit and that
neither dimension may substitute for the other.

FD-0018 established that epistemic eligibility does not create Procedural
Authority and that Procedural Authority does not create epistemic eligibility.

FD-0019 established how epistemic eligibility is legitimately demonstrated.

FD-0020 established that legitimate derived Procedural Authority requires a
valid, currently effective, and applicable authority grant traceable through
constituted governance to a constitutive authority source for the canonical
domain.

FD-0021 established that a constitutive authority source does not acquire
legitimate standing merely by declaring itself authoritative, possessing
technical control, originating or owning a resource, being recognized as
authoritative, acting first, or terminating an internal authority regress.

FD-0021 further established that constitutive standing must be grounded in a
relationship to the canonical domain sufficient under the normative framework
applicable to that domain to support the act of constituting governance.

FD-0022 established that a Discovery in Hold does not possess the canonical
standing conferred by Canonical Discovery Acceptance.

FD-0023 established the candidate finding that valid Canonical Discovery
Acceptance confers the standing required for a Discovery finding to serve as
an authoritative dependency in Stage engineering.

These findings expose an apparent bootstrap problem.

If an Accepted Discovery is required as an authoritative dependency for the
authority to perform Canonical Discovery Acceptance, then the first Acceptance
would appear to require a prior Accepted Discovery:

    Accepted Discovery
            │
            ▼
    Authority to Accept
            │
            ▼
    First Accepted Discovery

That relationship is circular.

The question is therefore:

> What independently legitimate authority can perform the first Canonical
> Discovery Acceptance before any Accepted Forge Discovery can serve as an
> authoritative dependency for that act?

---

# Discovery

The Procedural Authority required for Canonical Discovery Acceptance need not
originate from an already-Accepted Forge Discovery.

It may derive from a constitutive authority source possessing independently
grounded legitimate standing over the governed engineering domain under the
normative framework applicable to that domain.

That constitutive authority may establish, retain, or delegate bounded
Procedural Authority to perform Canonical Discovery Acceptance within its
legitimate standing and scope.

Therefore:

    Applicable Normative Framework
                │
                ▼
        Grounding Relationship
          to Governed Domain
                │
                ▼
        Legitimate Constitutive
             Standing
                │
                ▼
        Constituted Governance
                │
                ▼
        Bounded Procedural
             Authority
                │
                ▼
        Canonical Discovery
             Acceptance

An already-Accepted Discovery is therefore not the necessary ultimate source
of the Procedural Authority required for the first Canonical Discovery
Acceptance.

---

# Acceptance Authority Must Be Independently Grounded

The authority used to perform a Canonical Discovery Acceptance must not depend
upon the authoritative standing produced by that same Acceptance.

Therefore:

    Discovery D
        │
        ▼
    D claims Authority A exists
        │
        ▼
    A uses that claim
    to Accept D
        │
        ▼
    D becomes authoritative

is circular and does not establish legitimate Acceptance authority.

The governing invariant is:

> Acceptance Authority Must Not Depend Upon the Authoritative Standing
> Produced by That Same Acceptance.

The grounding of the Acceptance authority must be independently sufficient
without treating the finding undergoing Acceptance as already authoritative.

---

# The Constituted System Does Not Authorize Its Own Constitution

The first Acceptance does not require the Discovery-governance system to
create the authority necessary for its own constitution.

A constitutive authority source may possess legitimate standing relative to
the governed domain before the internal governance order exists.

Therefore:

    Prior Legitimate Standing
                │
                ▼
        Constitutive Act
                │
                ▼
        Governance Established
                │
                ▼
        Procedural Authority
                │
                ▼
        First Canonical Discovery
             Acceptance

The constituted governance system may subsequently regulate exercises of
derived Procedural Authority without being the ultimate source of the
constitutive standing from which that governance originated.

Therefore:

    Constituted Governance
                ≠
    Ultimate Source of Its
    Own Constitutive Standing

---

# Creation Does Not Establish Constitutive Standing

Creating a Method, artifact, repository, specification, project, or other
resource does not by itself establish legitimate constitutive standing over
every domain the created thing purports to govern.

Therefore:

    Creation
        ≠
    Legitimate Constitutive Standing

Likewise:

    Technical Control
        ≠
    Legitimate Constitutive Standing

and:

    Acting First
        ≠
    Legitimate Constitutive Standing

The constitutive authority source must possess the grounding relationship to
the governed domain required under the applicable normative framework.

This preserves the standing requirements established by FD-0021.

---

# Constitutive Authority Is Bounded by Scope

Legitimate constitutive standing over one domain does not automatically confer
authority over another domain.

Therefore:

    Authority Over Domain A
                ≠
    Authority Over Domain B

unless an independently sufficient grounding relationship establishes the
broader standing.

Constituted governance and derived Procedural Authority cannot legitimately
extend beyond the scope supported by their authority source.

Thus:

    Derived Authority
            ≤
    Constitutive Standing

A governance mechanism cannot enlarge its own authority merely by declaring a
larger scope.

---

# Delegation Cannot Amplify Authority

A constitutive authority source may delegate Procedural Authority to another
actor, office, mechanism, or governed process.

Such delegation cannot grant authority beyond that which the constitutive
source legitimately possesses and is permitted to delegate.

Therefore:

    Constitutive Authority
          Scope S
             │
             ▼
         Delegation
             │
             ▼
    Procedural Authority
          Scope ≤ S

Delegation transfers or assigns bounded authority.

It does not manufacture additional authority.

---

# Constitutive Authority May Retain Procedural Authority

Delegation is not required merely to create a formal separation between
constitutive and procedural roles.

A constitutive authority source may retain and directly exercise Procedural
Authority where that exercise:

- lies within its legitimate constitutive standing;
- is permitted by the applicable governance;
- satisfies the substantive requirements of the procedural act; and
- remains inspectably distinguishable as an exercise of Procedural Authority.

Therefore:

    Separation of Authority
            ≠
    Mandatory Separation of Persons

The same actor may occupy more than one authority role without collapsing the
roles themselves.

---

# Authority Does Not Create Epistemic Eligibility

Legitimate constitutive or procedural authority cannot make an epistemically
ineligible Discovery eligible merely by exercising authority over the
canonical Discovery domain.

Therefore:

    Procedural Authority
            ≠
    Epistemic Eligibility

and:

    Constitutive Authority
            ≠
    Truth-Making Authority

A Canonical Discovery Acceptance remains invalid if the applicable epistemic
requirements have not been satisfied, regardless of the strength of the
authority chain.

---

# Epistemic Eligibility Does Not Create Authority

The reverse is also true.

A Discovery may possess overwhelming evidentiary support, survive Forge and
Anvil examination, and satisfy every applicable epistemic requirement without
thereby granting anyone authority to alter its canonical standing.

Therefore:

    Epistemic Eligibility
            ≠
    Procedural Authority

A valid Canonical Discovery Acceptance requires both dimensions.

Neither may substitute for the other.

---

# Two Independent Chains Converge on Acceptance

Canonical Discovery Acceptance therefore depends upon two distinguishable
chains.

The epistemic chain establishes whether the finding is eligible for
Acceptance:

    Forge / Methodological Investigation
                    │
                    ▼
                  Anvil
                    │
                    ▼
        Epistemic Eligibility
                    │
                    │
                    └─────────────┐
                                  │
                                  ▼
                         Valid Canonical
                      Discovery Acceptance
                                  ▲
                                  │
                    ┌─────────────┘
                    │
        Applicable Normative Framework
                    │
                    ▼
        Grounding Relationship
                    │
                    ▼
        Constitutive Standing
                    │
                    ▼
        Procedural Authority

The governance chain establishes whether the canonical act is authorized.

Neither chain may substitute for the other.

Therefore:

    Epistemic Validity
            ≠
    Governance Authority

and:

    Governance Authority
            ≠
    Epistemic Validity

---

# The First Acceptance Is Not a Waiver

The absence of a finalized Canonical Discovery Acceptance specification does
not by itself eliminate the substantive conditions required for valid
Acceptance.

A first or pre-specification Acceptance is not valid merely because it is
historically first or because no formal procedure yet exists.

Therefore:

    First Acceptance
            ≠
    Waiver of Validity Requirements

and:

    Specification Absent
            ≠
    Authority Requirements Absent

A pre-specification Acceptance must still possess independently sufficient
epistemic and governance grounding.

A later specification may make those requirements operational, repeatable,
and inspectable.

It cannot create retroactive validity merely by declaring a prior act valid.

---

# Later Codification Does Not Retroactively Authorize

A specification written after an Acceptance may describe validity conditions
that already applied to the earlier act.

It may also provide a governed means of evaluating evidence concerning that
act.

It cannot make an originally unauthorized act authorized merely by later
declaration.

Therefore:

    Later Codification
            ≠
    Retroactive Authorization

Likewise, successful engineering results, consensus, historical use, technical
control, or later approval do not by themselves establish that an earlier
Acceptance was authorized when performed.

A later legitimate authority may perform a new valid Acceptance.

That later act does not transform an earlier invalid Acceptance into a valid
historical act.

---

# Evidence of Bootstrap Authority

A claim that a first or pre-specification Acceptance possessed legitimate
authority must be distinguishable from an unsupported assertion that such
authority existed.

Evidence sufficient for the applicable governance determination must permit
inspection of the relevant facts, including as applicable:

- the governed domain;
- the constitutive authority source;
- the grounding relationship to that domain;
- the applicable normative framework;
- the scope of constitutive standing;
- any authority grant or delegation;
- the Procedural Authority exercised;
- the scope of that procedural act; and
- the relationship between the authority basis and the Discovery being
  Accepted.

The exact evidence artifact, format, and procedure are not established here.

However:

    Authority Assertion
            ≠
    Authority Demonstration

A historical claim that valid authority existed cannot be treated as
demonstrated merely because the resulting Discovery has subsequently been
useful or widely relied upon.

---

# Independent Grounding Prevents Bootstrap Circularity

The first Acceptance becomes non-circular when its authority chain does not
depend upon the canonical standing that the Acceptance itself creates.

The valid structural relationship is:

    Applicable Normative Framework
                │
                ▼
        Grounding Relationship
                │
                ▼
        Constitutive Standing
                │
                ▼
        Governance / Procedural
             Authority
                │
                ▼
        Canonical Discovery
             Acceptance
                │
                ▼
        First Accepted Discovery

The invalid circular relationship is:

    Discovery Requires Acceptance
                │
                ▼
    Discovery Finding Supplies
    Authority for Acceptance
                │
                ▼
    Acceptance Makes Discovery
    Finding Authoritative
                │
                └──────────────► back to authority

The first structure derives authority from a source logically independent of
the standing produced by the Acceptance.

The second does not.

---

# Framework Applicability Remains a Dependency

This Discovery requires evaluation under the normative framework applicable to
the governed domain.

It does not establish that an actor may select whichever normative framework
produces the desired authority result.

Therefore:

    Desired Authority
            │
            ▼
    Convenient Framework Selection
            │
            X
            ▼
    Legitimate Standing

is not established.

The method by which normative-framework applicability is determined remains a
separate dependency unless subsequent engineering demonstrates that it must be
resolved here.

---

# Governance Amendment Remains a Dependency

This Discovery establishes a possible legitimate source and derivation of the
Procedural Authority required for initial Canonical Discovery Acceptance.

It does not establish:

- how constituted governance may later be amended;
- who may amend it;
- whether constitutive authority may alter procedural requirements during an
  active Acceptance;
- how temporal applicability of governance rules is established; or
- how replacement of a constitutive authority source is governed.

Those questions remain separate unless subsequent engineering demonstrates
that they block the current engineering transition.

---

# Consequence

The first Canonical Discovery Acceptance does not require circular reliance
upon an already-Accepted Discovery for the authority that performs the act.

Instead:

    Independently Grounded
    Constitutive Standing
            │
            ▼
    Constituted Governance
            │
            ▼
    Bounded Procedural Authority
            │
            ▼
    Canonical Discovery Acceptance
            │
            ▼
    First Accepted Discovery

while independently:

    Forge / Investigation
            │
            ▼
          Anvil
            │
            ▼
    Epistemic Eligibility
            │
            ▼
    Canonical Discovery Acceptance

Both chains must converge upon the Acceptance.

The existence of one does not cure failure of the other.

---

# Does Not Establish

This Discovery does not establish:

- one universal normative framework applicable to every canonical domain;
- how the applicable normative framework is selected or determined;
- the ultimate legitimacy of every normative framework;
- that creation, ownership, technical control, historical priority, consensus,
  or successful outcomes independently establish constitutive standing;
- one universal grounding relationship sufficient for every domain;
- that constitutive authority is unlimited;
- that constitutive authority over one domain establishes authority over
  another;
- that delegation may exceed the authority possessed by the delegating source;
- that constitutive and procedural authority must be exercised by different
  persons;
- that constitutive or Procedural Authority creates epistemic eligibility;
- that epistemic eligibility creates Procedural Authority;
- that the first Acceptance may waive substantive validity requirements;
- that absence of a finalized Acceptance specification makes every historical
  Acceptance valid;
- that later codification retroactively authorizes an earlier invalid act;
- the complete evidence format required to demonstrate bootstrap authority;
- the complete Canonical Discovery Acceptance procedure;
- the complete governance-amendment procedure;
- the procedure for replacing or superseding constitutive authority;
- that any particular person, office, mechanism, or process presently
  possesses constitutive or Procedural Authority over the Forge–Stage Method;
- or that this Discovery is itself Accepted.

---

# Open Dependencies

This Discovery exposes but does not resolve the following engineering
questions:

1. What normative framework is applicable to the Forge–Stage Method's
   canonical Discovery domain, and how is that applicability established?

2. What grounding relationship establishes the actual constitutive authority
   source for that domain?

3. What evidence demonstrates the resulting constitutive standing and any
   retained or delegated Procedural Authority?

4. What governance applies to later amendment, replacement, revocation, or
   succession of constitutive and Procedural Authority?

These questions become blocking only where subsequent engineering demonstrates
that their resolution is required.
