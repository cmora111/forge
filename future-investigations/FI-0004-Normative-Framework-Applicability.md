# FI-0004 — Normative Framework Applicability

**Status:** Deferred  
**Origin:** FD-0025 — Constitutive Authority for Initial Canonical Discovery Acceptance  
**Deferred:** 2026-08-30

---

## Question

What makes a normative framework applicable to a Canonical Discovery
Governance Domain?

---

## Why Deferred

The investigation of FD-0025 established that Procedural Authority for
Canonical Discovery Acceptance may derive from a constitutive authority source
possessing independently grounded legitimate standing over the governed
engineering domain under the normative framework applicable to that domain.

This exposed a deeper dependency:

What makes that normative framework applicable to the governed domain in the
first place?

The Forge investigated this question far enough to reject several insufficient
bases for framework applicability and to develop a candidate account based upon
a grounded constitutive relationship, bounded scope, broader applicable
constraints, and continuing standing.

The investigation also exposed deeper questions concerning the exact nature of
the Canonical Discovery Governance Domain, the sufficiency of constitutive
grounding, framework applicability over time, and the relationship between
internal governance and broader normative constraints.

Further resolution of those questions was not demonstrated to be necessary to
resolve the engineering dependency that had brought the work to the Forge.

Continuing the investigation would therefore have extended dependency tracing
beyond the dependency presently blocking the engineering work.

The investigation was deliberately stopped and preserved for future re-entry.

This Future Investigation records that work without promoting its candidate
conclusions to Accepted Forge Discovery findings.

## Preserved Investigative Work

The following material records the state reached by the Forge investigation
when it was deferred.

Unless independently established by an authoritative Forge Discovery, material

### Insufficient Bases for Framework Applicability

The investigation rejected several propositions as independently sufficient
bases for normative-framework applicability.

#### Declaration

A declaration that a framework governs a domain does not establish that the
framework actually applies to that domain.

    Framework Declaration ≠ Framework Applicability

---

#### Framework Self-Assertion

A framework cannot establish its own applicability merely by containing a rule
stating that it applies.

    Framework Self-Assertion ≠ Framework Applicability

Otherwise the applicability question becomes circular.

---

#### Domain Creation

Creation of a domain does not independently grant unrestricted authority to
select any normative framework for that domain.

    Domain Creation ≠ Unrestricted Framework-Selection Authority

Creation may provide evidence relevant to the relationship between an actor and
a domain without itself establishing the complete legitimacy or scope of
constitutive standing.

---

#### Historical Use

Repeated or longstanding use of a framework does not independently establish
its applicability.

    Historical Use ≠ Framework Applicability

Historical use may nevertheless provide evidence concerning an established
governance relationship.

---

#### Repository Membership

The physical or logical location of an artifact does not determine which
normative framework governs it.

    Repository Membership ≠ Normative Framework Applicability

The governed domain must be identified independently of storage location.

---

#### Voluntary Agreement

Voluntary agreement may contribute to the constitution of governance within a
bounded domain.

It does not establish unlimited authority over third parties, independent
domains, or matters outside the scope supported by the constitutive
relationship.

    Voluntary Agreement ≠ Unlimited Framework Applicability

---

#### Participation

Participation may establish or contribute to a governance relationship within
the scope of that participation.

It does not establish unlimited submission to governance.

    Participation ≠ Unlimited Submission to Governance

and:

    Method Use ≠ Governance Participation

---

#### Relationship Existence

The mere existence of a relationship to a domain does not establish that the
relationship is normatively sufficient to constitute governance over every
aspect of that domain.

    Relationship Existence ≠ Normative Sufficiency

Creation, authorship, ownership, technical control, participation, or voluntary
agreement may provide evidence relevant to grounding without independently
establishing unlimited constitutive standing.

---

## Circularity and Regress

The investigation identified a circularity risk in the relationship between a
normative framework and its grounding relationship.

If:

    Grounding is sufficient because Framework F says it is

while:

    Framework F applies because that grounding is sufficient

then framework applicability and grounding depend upon each other without an
independent basis.

The investigation therefore rejected simple mutual self-justification between
framework and grounding.

At the same time, requiring every normative framework to derive its
applicability from another framework of the same kind produces a regress.

Therefore, the investigation preserved the distinction:

    Framework Applicability Does Not Necessarily Require
    Authorization by Another Framework of the Same Kind

A possible stopping point was identified in the constitutive relationship
between the actor or actors and the bounded domain being governed, subject to
broader normative constraints already applicable to that relationship or
constitutive act.

The exact conditions sufficient to establish such grounding remain unresolved.

---

## Internal and Broader Normative Order

The investigation distinguished an internal normative framework from broader
normative constraints that may already apply to the actors, domain, or
constitutive act.

The resulting structural model was:

    Broader Applicable
    Normative Constraints
             │
             ▼
    Constitutive Relationship
    Actors ↔ Governed Domain
             │
             ▼
    Internal Normative
    Framework
             │
             ▼
    Internal Governance

An internal governance framework cannot obtain unlimited authority merely by
agreement among its participants where broader applicable constraints already
limit what those participants may constitute.

At the same time, the absence of superior internal governance does not make
voluntary constitution impossible.

The investigation therefore preserved:

    Absence of Superior Internal Governance
    ≠ Absence of Normative Constraint

and:

    Absence of Superior Internal Governance
    ≠ Impossibility of Voluntary Constitution

---

## Bounded Constitution

The investigation found no requirement that voluntary constitution necessarily
involve multiple actors.

A single actor may potentially constitute governance over a bounded domain
where that actor possesses a sufficient grounding relationship to the domain.

Therefore:

    Plurality of Actors ≠ Necessary Condition of Constitution

This does not establish unlimited authority merely because the actor is
governing a domain with which that actor has a relationship.

In particular:

    Self-Governance ≠ Truth-Making Authority

and:

    Creation ≠ Unlimited Constitutive Standing

The scope of constituted governance cannot exceed the scope supported by the
grounding relationship.

Therefore:

    Constituted Scope ≤ Grounded Scope

Constitution by participating actors also does not establish governance
authority over independent nonparticipants.

    Constitution by Participants ≠ Authority Over Nonparticipants

---

## Methodological Lineage and Governance

The investigation distinguished continued use or derivation of a Method from
continued participation in the same governance domain.

A fork, derivative implementation, or independent use may preserve
methodological lineage without preserving governance jurisdiction.

Therefore:

    Methodological Lineage ≠ Governance Jurisdiction

and:

    Method Use ≠ Governance Participation

Similarly, later expansion of a Method does not automatically expand the scope
of the constitutive relationship that originally grounded its governance.

    Original Domain Scope ≠ Automatically Present Domain Scope

---

## Temporal Applicability

The investigation also tested whether an initially sufficient grounding
relationship would make a framework permanently applicable.

It did not.

Therefore:

    Prior Framework Applicability ≠ Perpetual Framework Applicability

and:

    Prior Grounding ≠ Permanent Grounding

A material change to the grounding relationship may require reevaluation of
present framework applicability or scope.

However, not every changed circumstance automatically terminates framework
applicability.

Therefore:

    Changed Circumstances ≠ Automatic Loss of Framework Applicability

Historical possession of constitutive or delegated authority does not establish
present possession of that authority.

    Historical Constitutive Standing ≠ Present Constitutive Standing

and:

    Historical Possession of Authority ≠ Present Possession of Authority

Present framework scope likewise cannot be established solely from historical
scope.

    Present Framework Scope Cannot Be Established Solely by Historical Scope

---

## Historical and Present Applicability

Loss of present applicability does not, by itself, retroactively destroy
framework applicability that legitimately existed previously.

Therefore:

    Loss of Present Applicability
    ≠ Retroactive Loss of Historical Applicability

Conversely, restoration or establishment of present applicability does not
establish that the framework continuously applied throughout an earlier period.

    Present Applicability
    ≠ Retroactive Continuous Applicability

Continuing applicability also need not be continually recreated in the absence
of a material change requiring reevaluation.

    Continuing Validity ≠ Continuous Re-establishment

---

## Preserved Candidate Finding

The investigation reached the following candidate formulation before further
work was deferred:

> A normative framework does not become applicable to a governance domain
> merely because the framework declares itself applicable. Its applicability
> must be grounded in a sufficient relationship between the constituting actor
> or actors and the domain being constituted, within the scope supported by
> that relationship and subject to broader normative constraints applicable to
> the domain, actors, or constitutive act.
>
> For a voluntarily constituted governance domain, one or more actors may
> establish an internal normative framework where their grounding relationship
> is sufficient to constitute governance over that bounded domain. Such
> constitution does not thereby establish authority over independent persons,
> domains, implementations, or matters outside the grounded scope.
>
> Framework applicability and its scope remain dependent upon the continuing
> standing of the grounding relationships upon which they rely. A material
> change to those relationships may therefore alter or terminate present
> applicability or scope, but does not by itself retroactively invalidate
> governance that was legitimate while the required grounding existed.
> Continuing validity does not require continual reconstitution absent a change
> requiring reevaluation.

This formulation is preserved as investigative work.

Its preservation in this Future Investigation does not establish it as an
Accepted Forge Discovery finding.

                │
                ▼

                │
                │

                │

    the claimed standing and scope?

This model is preserved to prevent future investigation from unnecessarily

It is not established here as a normative Stage model.

## Canonical Discovery Governance Domain

The investigation also exposed the need to distinguish the Canonical Discovery
Governance Domain from the repository or the entire Forge–Stage Method.

A candidate distinction developed during the investigation was:

> The canonical domain relevant to Canonical Discovery Acceptance is not the
> repository, the physical collection of Discovery artifacts, unrestricted
> Forge investigative process, the entire Stage Method, or every independent
> implementation.
>
> It is the bounded governance domain within which canonical standing of Forge
> Discovery findings is established, maintained, and changed for authoritative
> use by the governed Forge–Stage Method instance.
>
> Participation in that governance domain does not itself confer Accepted
> standing, epistemic authority, or authoritative dependency standing.

The associated candidate distinctions were:

    Repository ≠ Canonical Discovery Governance Domain

    Investigation ≠ Canonical Discovery Governance Domain

    Governed Discovery ≠ Accepted Discovery

    Canonical Governance Authority ≠ Epistemic Authority

    Membership in Governance Domain ≠ Possession of Accepted Standing

The exact definition and standing of the Canonical Discovery Governance Domain
were not established by a separate Discovery before this investigation was
deferred.

This material is therefore preserved as a candidate distinction rather than an
authoritative definition.

## Unresolved Questions

This Future Investigation preserves, without requiring immediate resolution,
questions including:

1. What exact conditions make a constitutive relationship sufficient to ground
   applicability of an internal normative framework?

2. How is the relevant governed domain identified independently of the
   framework claiming authority over it?

3. What exact Canonical Discovery Governance Domain exists within a governed
   Forge–Stage Method instance?

4. What broader normative constraints apply to a particular constitutive act,
   and how is their applicability established without recreating the same
   circularity or regress?

5. What evidence is sufficient to demonstrate the grounding relationship
   supporting framework applicability?

6. How should changes to a grounding relationship affect present framework
   applicability or scope?

7. When does a changed circumstance become material enough to require
   reevaluation of framework applicability?

8. How should later participants, implementations, forks, or derivative Methods
   be distinguished with respect to governance participation and jurisdiction?

These questions are preserved without assuming that each requires a separate
Forge Discovery.

Governance amendment, replacement, revocation, and succession are preserved
separately rather than expanded here.

---

## Re-entry Condition

Reopen this investigation only when a concrete Forge–Stage engineering
operation requires resolution of normative-framework applicability,
constitutive grounding, governed-domain scope, or continuing applicability in
order for that operation to proceed legitimately.

A theoretical possibility that framework applicability could be questioned is
not sufficient reason for re-entry.

Further investigation should be limited to the dependency demonstrated to be
blocking the engineering operation that caused re-entry.
