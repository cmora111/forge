# FI-0005 — Governance Amendment, Revocation, and Succession

**Status:** Deferred  
**Origin:** FD-0025 — Constitutive Authority for Initial Canonical Discovery Acceptance  
**Deferred:** 2026-08-30

---

## Question

How may constituted Forge–Stage governance be legitimately amended, replaced,
revoked, or succeeded after its initial establishment?

---

## Why Deferred

The investigation of FD-0025 required determining whether the Procedural
Authority necessary for initial Canonical Discovery Acceptance could arise
without already depending upon an Accepted Forge Discovery.

That investigation established a candidate authority structure in which bounded
Procedural Authority may derive from legitimately grounded constitutive
standing over the governed engineering domain.

Once constitutive standing and derived authority were considered over time,
additional questions became visible concerning what happens when:

- constituted governance is amended;
- a constitutive authority source changes or ceases to possess relevant
  standing;
- delegated Procedural Authority is modified or revoked;
- one governance arrangement replaces another;
- responsibility for governance passes to a successor;
- the governed domain materially changes; or
- historical governance acts must be distinguished from present authority.

Those questions concern the continuity and change of governance after its
establishment.

Resolution of them was not demonstrated to be necessary to establish the
initial constitutive-authority finding investigated by FD-0025.

They were therefore deferred rather than extending that investigation into a
complete governance lifecycle.

This investigation is preserved for future work and is not presently blocking
the engineering work that exposed it.

---

## Preserved Investigative Work

The following material preserves distinctions exposed during the investigation
that led to this Future Investigation.

Unless independently established elsewhere by an authoritative Forge
Discovery, preservation here does not confer additional epistemic, procedural,
normative, or canonical authority upon the material.

---

### Derived Authority

Procedural Authority derived from constitutive standing cannot exceed the
standing from which it derives.

Therefore:

    Derived Authority ≤ Constitutive Standing

Delegation cannot amplify the authority possessed by the delegating source.

A governance mechanism therefore cannot obtain broader authority merely by
passing authority through additional offices, actors, procedures, or
delegations.

---

### Historical and Present Authority

Possession of authority at an earlier time does not, by itself, establish
possession of that authority now.

Therefore:

    Historical Possession of Authority ≠ Present Possession of Authority

and:

    Historical Constitutive Standing ≠ Present Constitutive Standing

Changes to the relationships grounding constitutive standing may affect present
authority without necessarily invalidating governance acts that were legitimate
when performed.

---

### Loss of Present Standing

Loss of present governance standing does not, by itself, establish that the
same governance lacked standing historically.

Therefore:

    Loss of Present Applicability
    ≠ Retroactive Loss of Historical Applicability

A governance act must be evaluated according to the authority and conditions
applicable to the act when it occurred.

---

### Restoration or New Standing

Present possession or restoration of authority does not establish that the same
authority existed continuously throughout an earlier period.

Therefore:

    Present Applicability
    ≠ Retroactive Continuous Applicability

A later governance arrangement cannot manufacture uninterrupted historical
authority merely by asserting continuity.

---

### Governance Continuity

Continuing governance need not be continually recreated merely because time
passes.

Therefore:

    Continuing Validity ≠ Continuous Re-establishment

Where the relationships supporting governance remain materially unchanged,
continued governance may remain effective without repeated constitution.

This does not establish that governance remains valid after a material change
to the relationships upon which its standing depends.

---

### Changed Circumstances

Not every change affecting a governed Method, its participants, its artifacts,
or its implementation automatically terminates existing governance.

Therefore:

    Changed Circumstances ≠ Automatic Loss of Governance Standing

A material change may nevertheless require reevaluation of the standing, scope,
delegation, or governance arrangement affected by that change.

The criteria for determining materiality remain unresolved.

---

### Governance Scope

Historical governance scope does not automatically establish present governance
scope.

Therefore:

    Original Grounded Scope ≠ Automatically Present Domain Scope

and:

    Present Framework Scope Cannot Be Established Solely by Historical Scope

Expansion of a Method, governance domain, participant population,
implementation environment, or engineering responsibility may expose questions
that were outside the scope of the original constitutive grounding.

---

### Amendment

A constituted governance system may potentially continue through legitimate
change rather than requiring complete reconstitution whenever one of its rules
changes.

The investigation therefore preserved the candidate distinction:

    Governance Change ≠ Necessarily Governance Replacement

and:

    Framework Applicability May Continue Through Legitimate Governance Change

However, the investigation did not establish what makes an amendment
legitimate, who possesses amendment authority, what limits apply to that
authority, or when an amendment becomes a replacement of the governing
arrangement rather than a continuation of it.

---

### Revocation

Delegated authority cannot be assumed to continue merely because it once
existed.

The investigation exposed the need to distinguish:

- the standing of the constitutive source;
- the authority originally delegated;
- the continuing validity of that delegation; and
- the authority presently exercised.

The exact conditions under which delegated Procedural Authority may be revoked,
expires, or otherwise ceases remain unresolved.

---

### Succession

Method continuity does not automatically establish continuity of governance
authority.

Therefore:

    Method Continuity ≠ Governance-Scope Continuity

and:

    Methodological Lineage ≠ Governance Jurisdiction

A successor may preserve artifacts, practices, terminology, or methodological
lineage without necessarily inheriting the governance standing possessed by a
predecessor.

The conditions under which governance authority may legitimately pass to a
successor remain unresolved.

---

### Authority Laundering

Historical authority cannot be used merely as a label to manufacture present
authority after the grounding that supported the historical authority has
ceased.

Likewise, succession, delegation, amendment, or continuity claims cannot
increase authority beyond the standing legitimately supporting them.

Therefore:

    Historical Authority ≠ Automatic Present Authority

and:

    Succession ≠ Automatic Authority Inheritance

These distinctions prevent governance continuity from becoming a mechanism for
authority amplification or authority laundering.

---

## Unresolved Questions

This Future Investigation preserves the following unresolved questions:

1. What authority is required to amend constituted Forge–Stage governance?

2. What limits constrain amendment authority?

3. When does a governance change constitute an amendment of existing governance
   rather than replacement by a new governance arrangement?

4. What conditions permit revocation of delegated Procedural Authority?

5. Does delegated authority require an explicit duration, or may it continue
   until legitimately revoked or otherwise terminated?

6. What happens when the constitutive standing supporting a delegation
   materially changes or ceases to exist?

7. What conditions permit governance authority to pass to a successor actor,
   office, organization, or governance arrangement?

8. What evidence is sufficient to demonstrate legitimate governance
   succession?

9. How should historical governance acts be treated after the governing
   authority that performed them has been replaced or revoked?

10. How should changes in the scope of the governed domain affect existing
    constitutive standing and delegated authority?

11. What constitutes a material change requiring reevaluation of governance
    standing or scope?

12. How should periods in which governance authority cannot be demonstrated be
    treated when later governance standing is established?

These questions are preserved without assuming that each requires a separate
Forge Discovery, Stage specification, or governance mechanism.

## Boundary with FI-0004

FI-0004 — Normative Framework Applicability preserves the investigation of what
makes a normative framework applicable to a bounded governance domain,
including the grounding and scope supporting that applicability.

This Future Investigation addresses a different question:

    FI-0004:
    What grounds the applicability of the governance framework?

    FI-0005:
    How does legitimately constituted governance change over time?

The investigations may eventually interact, particularly where a change in
grounding affects continuing governance standing.

That interaction does not require them to be investigated together unless
subsequent engineering demonstrates that both questions have become blocking.

---

## Re-entry Condition

Reopen this investigation only when a concrete Forge–Stage engineering
operation requires governance to be amended, replaced, revoked, transferred,
or succeeded, or when a material change makes the continuing standing or scope
of existing governance necessary to determine.

The theoretical possibility of future governance change is not sufficient
reason for re-entry.

Further investigation should be limited to the governance-change dependency
demonstrated to be blocking the engineering operation that caused re-entry.
