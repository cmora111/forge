# Governance

## Purpose

The `governance` directory contains records and candidate instruments that
concern the prospective governance of engineering work.

This directory has one exclusive responsibility:

> Preserve governance instruments together with their authority and status
> boundaries.

Directory placement does not create authority, enact an instrument, establish
a procedure, or change the status of an engineering artifact.

This README is a non-operative directory guide. It describes repository
conventions but does not govern any instrument or supply authority.

---

## Scope

Candidate or operative governance instruments may address:

- the domain governed by an arrangement;
- the source and limits of constituted authority;
- the identity and powers of an authorized holder;
- procedures expressly authorized within a governed domain;
- enactment and prospective effective boundaries; and
- bounded maintenance of an established arrangement.

Files maintained in this directory should not imply authority that has not
been independently established.

### `candidates/`

Contains proposed governance instruments that have not been constituted or
enacted.

A candidate remains non-operative unless its own valid constitutive
requirements are deliberately completed.

Creation, inspection, preservation, download, revision, repository placement,
or Git commit does not change candidate status.

---

## Governance Instrument Conventions

Candidate and operative governance instruments maintained here are expected
to:

- identify its status;
- identify its governed domain;
- state the grounding and limits of its authority;
- distinguish inspection from authorization and enactment;
- state whether and how it may become operative;
- preserve prospective and historical boundaries; and
- avoid claiming authority over persons or work outside its established scope.

These instruments should not:

- manufacture authority through documentation alone;
- treat Anvil inspection as a constitutive act;
- acquire authority from repository ownership or file placement;
- silently amend an existing governance arrangement;
- give retroactive effect to a prospective act; or
- merge historically distinct authority chains by similarity or implication.

---

## Candidate Boundary

Candidate instruments are proposals, not governing authority.

The word `CANDIDATE` should appear in the filename of each candidate instrument
unless another authoritative naming rule requires a stronger distinction.

Candidate language should identify any act required for constitution or
enactment. Until that act occurs, all holder identities, procedures, powers,
and effects described by the candidate remain proposed.

A candidate that survives Anvil inspection has survived an epistemic test. It
has not thereby been authorized, constituted, or enacted.

---

## Authority

Candidate and operative governance instruments derive authority only from a
valid constitutive or enactment basis that is independently established and
preserved.

The `governance` directory and this README do not supply that basis.

If an instrument conflicts with an authority that validly governs its domain,
that independently established authority controls within its domain. This
README does not determine that authority relationship.

---

## Change Discipline

Changes to a governance artifact may alter its meaning even when they appear
editorial.

Proposed changes should therefore be reviewed for:

- consistency with the artifact's established authority;
- preservation of its governed domain and constitutive limits;
- unintended enlargement of holder powers;
- retroactive or external-jurisdiction effects;
- effects on voluntary participation and freedom of exit; and
- preservation of the distinction between inspection and enactment.

Requirements for changing an operative instrument arise only from its
independently established authority. This README does not authorize a change
mechanism.

A candidate may be revised without governance effect, but each revision should
continue to identify itself as non-operative.

## Relationship to Other Forge Artifacts


| Artifact | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Constitutional records | Establish or preserve foundational authority |
| Forge Decisions | Record bounded findings and decisions |
| Anvil inspections | Test claims, evidence, and boundaries |
| Governance candidates | Propose a prospective governance arrangement |
| Operative instruments | Govern only through valid constituted authority |


Historical evidence may inform a governance instrument without becoming
governed by it.

The current candidate-review workflow is:

```text
Independent Grounding
        ↓
Candidate Instrument
        ↓
Anvil Inspection
        ↓
Deliberate Constitutive Act
        ↓
Prospective Governance Effect
```

This workflow describes the review path used for the current candidate. It is
not a universal constitutive sequence.

Anvil inspection tests the candidate. It neither supplies authority nor
constitutes or enacts an arrangement. A deliberate constitutive act creates
prospective governance effect only when its basis is validly available and its
requirements are completed.
