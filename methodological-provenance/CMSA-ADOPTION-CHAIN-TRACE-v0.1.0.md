## Objective and method

Trace the constitutional adoption chain and look throughout both available
archives for an independently established present power allowing CMSA to
authorize binding subordinate procedures. Inspect operative language separately
from the historical act establishing its force and its present applicability. A
filename, self-declared status, discovery finding, or retrospective summary is
not itself proof of adoption. Search performed across archive text files,
including constitutional, authority, lifecycle, history, record, and governance
filenames; keyword matches were triaged and relevant documents inspected.
Negative result is bounded to these snapshots and this search, not proof of
historical nonexistence.

## Search results and limits

A broad text search of all text-like entries in the two snapshots for adoption
records, ratification, approval-by, authorization declarations, and
amendment-authority language did not surface an independently identifiable act
granting CMSA general subordinate-procedure-authorizing discretion. Most
relevant hits point to explicit reservations in FD-0025, FD-0033, FI-0005, and
the Candidate constitutional law. The absence of a matching keyword is not
proof no relevant act exists in an image, opaque archive entry, alternate
terminology, other repository, or later version. No source hash comparison with
the current worktree was possible.

## Competing readings

**Reading A: retained constitutive discretion.** The historical constituting
sequence established governance, so its source may have retained a bounded
rule-making function. Counter: FD-0037 explicitly refuses to establish every
CMSA power; neither retained power nor its scope is identified in an operative
adopted clause.

**Reading B: delegated procedure authorization.** Applicable governance could
delegate a bounded function to CMSA or Stage PA. Counter: FD-0030 allows such
topology but does not establish a particular delegation; FD-0027's model is not
a grant.

**Reading C: existing lifecycle adoption machinery.** The accepted lifecycle
describes adoption and specifications. Counter: it explicitly requires an
independently authorized holder and constituting source; its procedure cannot
grant itself authority.

**Reading D: prospective constitutional amendment.** An amendment might
establish a missing power going forward. Counter: FI-0005 expressly leaves
amendment holder and procedure unresolved; an amendment cannot be performed
merely because it would solve the current problem.

**Reading E: existing administrative implementation only.** Nonbinding aids may
record existing decisions without asserting new governance. Counter: this route
cannot confer governing force on CDA or a Freeze procedure.
